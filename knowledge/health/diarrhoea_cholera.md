# Diarrhoea and Cholera Prevention and Treatment

## What is Diarrhoea?

Diarrhoea is passing loose or watery stools 3 or more times in a day. It is one of the leading causes of death in children under 5 in Nigeria, mainly due to dehydration (loss of body water and salts).

## Common Causes

- Contaminated water and food
- Poor hygiene (not washing hands)
- Bacterial infections (cholera, typhoid, E. coli)
- Viral infections (rotavirus — common in children)
- Parasites
- Food poisoning

## Symptoms

- Frequent loose or watery stools
- Stomach cramps
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fever (sometimes)
- Blood in stool (in some cases — see a doctor immediately)
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, sunken eyes, little or no urine, crying without tears, weakness

## Treatment of Diarrhoea

### Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)

ORS is the most important treatment for diarrhoea. It replaces the water and salts lost from the body.

**How to prepare ORS:**
1. Wash your hands with soap and clean water
2. Pour the contents of one ORS sachet into a clean container
3. Add 1 litre (about 4 cups) of clean drinking water
4. Stir until completely dissolved
5. Give the solution to the patient in small, frequent sips

**If you do not have ORS sachets, make a home solution:**
1. Wash your hands
2. Mix 6 level teaspoons of sugar and half a level teaspoon of salt in 1 litre of clean water
3. Stir well and give in small sips

**How much ORS to give:**
- Children under 2 years: 50-100ml (quarter to half a cup) after each loose stool
- Children 2-10 years: 100-200ml (half to one cup) after each loose stool
- Older children and adults: as much as they want after each loose stool

### Zinc Supplements (for Children)

- Give zinc tablets along with ORS for children under 5 with diarrhoea
- Children under 6 months: 10mg zinc daily for 10-14 days
- Children 6 months to 5 years: 20mg zinc daily for 10-14 days
- Zinc reduces the duration and severity of diarrhoea and prevents future episodes
- Continue for the full 10-14 days even if diarrhoea stops

### Continue Feeding

- Continue breastfeeding if the child is breastfeeding
- Continue normal feeding for older children and adults
- Give small, frequent meals
- Avoid sugary drinks and fruit juices (they can worsen diarrhoea)

## When to Go to the Hospital

Go to the hospital immediately if:
- Diarrhoea lasts more than 3 days
- There is blood in the stool
- The patient cannot drink or keep fluids down (vomiting everything)
- Signs of severe dehydration: sunken eyes, very dry mouth, skin that stays pinched, no urine for 6+ hours, very weak or unconscious
- High fever
- The patient is a child under 6 months
- The patient is pregnant

## Cholera

### What is Cholera?
Cholera is a severe form of diarrhoea caused by contaminated water or food. It can kill within hours if not treated. Cholera outbreaks are common in Nigeria, especially during the rainy season.

### Symptoms of Cholera
- Sudden onset of profuse, watery diarrhoea (often described as "rice water" stool)
- Severe vomiting
- Rapid dehydration
- Leg cramps
- No fever in most cases

### What to Do
- Start ORS immediately — do not wait
- Go to the nearest hospital or cholera treatment centre as fast as possible
- Cholera can kill within hours — speed is critical
- Report suspected cholera cases to your Local Government health authority

### How to Prevent Cholera
- Drink only clean, treated, or boiled water
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet
- Cook food thoroughly and eat it while hot
- Wash fruits and vegetables with clean water
- Use a latrine or toilet — do not defecate in the open
- Dispose of waste properly
- Avoid buying food from unhygienic vendors during outbreaks

## Prevention of Diarrhoea

### Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH)

1. **Safe Water**
   - Boil water for at least 1 minute before drinking, or
   - Use water purification tablets (Water Guard, Aqua Tabs), or
   - Use a water filter
   - Store clean water in covered containers

2. **Handwashing**
   - Wash hands with soap and water:
     - Before preparing or eating food
     - After using the toilet
     - After changing a baby's nappy
     - After touching animals
   - If soap is not available, use ash

3. **Food Safety**
   - Cook food thoroughly
   - Eat food while it is still hot
   - Wash fruits and vegetables before eating
   - Keep food covered and away from flies
   - Do not eat food that smells bad or looks spoiled

4. **Sanitation**
   - Use a toilet or latrine — do not defecate in the open or near water sources
   - Dispose of children's faeces in a toilet
   - Keep your environment clean

### Vaccination

- Rotavirus vaccine is part of Nigeria's routine immunisation schedule for infants (at 6 and 10 weeks)
- Cholera vaccine is available during outbreaks — follow government health announcements

## Tips

- Always keep ORS sachets at home — they are cheap and can save lives
- Do not give anti-diarrhoeal drugs (like Imodium/Loperamide) to children under 12
- Do not give antibiotics unless prescribed by a doctor
- Diarrhoea in children kills through dehydration — replacing fluids is the priority
- If your community has a cholera outbreak, report to the nearest health centre immediately
