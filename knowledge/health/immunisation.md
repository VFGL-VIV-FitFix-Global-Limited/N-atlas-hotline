# Childhood Immunisation Schedule in Nigeria

## Why Immunisation Matters

Immunisation (vaccination) protects your child from dangerous and deadly diseases. Vaccines are safe, effective, and free at government health centres in Nigeria. Many children in Nigeria die from diseases that could have been prevented by vaccination.

## Nigeria's Routine Immunisation Schedule

| Age | Vaccine | Disease Prevented |
|---|---|---|
| At birth | BCG | Tuberculosis |
| At birth | OPV 0 (oral polio) | Polio |
| At birth | Hepatitis B birth dose | Hepatitis B |
| 6 weeks | OPV 1 | Polio |
| 6 weeks | Pentavalent 1 (DPT-HepB-Hib) | Diphtheria, Pertussis (whooping cough), Tetanus, Hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b |
| 6 weeks | PCV 1 (Pneumococcal) | Pneumonia, meningitis |
| 6 weeks | Rotavirus 1 | Rotavirus diarrhoea |
| 6 weeks | IPV 1 (injectable polio) | Polio |
| 10 weeks | OPV 2 | Polio |
| 10 weeks | Pentavalent 2 | Same as above |
| 10 weeks | PCV 2 | Pneumonia, meningitis |
| 10 weeks | Rotavirus 2 | Rotavirus diarrhoea |
| 14 weeks | OPV 3 | Polio |
| 14 weeks | Pentavalent 3 | Same as above |
| 14 weeks | PCV 3 | Pneumonia, meningitis |
| 14 weeks | IPV 2 | Polio |
| 6 months | Vitamin A (1st dose) | Vitamin A deficiency |
| 9 months | Measles 1 | Measles |
| 9 months | Yellow Fever | Yellow fever |
| 9 months | Meningitis A (MenA) | Meningitis A |
| 12 months | Vitamin A (2nd dose) | Vitamin A deficiency |
| 15 months | Measles 2 | Measles |

Vitamin A supplementation continues every 6 months until age 5.

## Where to Get Immunised

- Primary Health Centres (PHCs) — in every Local Government Area
- General and teaching hospitals
- Immunisation outreach sessions in communities
- National Immunisation Plus Days (NIPDs) — announced by government

All routine immunisations are **FREE** at government health centres.

## What to Bring

- Child's immunisation card (given at birth or first visit)
- If you have lost the card, the health worker can start a new one — do not skip vaccination because of a lost card

## Common Concerns

### "My child had a reaction last time"
- Mild reactions are normal: slight fever, swelling at injection site, fussiness
- Give paracetamol for fever
- Severe reactions (difficulty breathing, high fever, severe swelling) are very rare — take the child to the hospital
- Mild reactions are NOT a reason to skip future vaccines

### "My child is sick — can they still be vaccinated?"
- Children with mild illness (cold, cough, mild diarrhoea) can still be vaccinated
- If the child has a high fever or is seriously ill, wait until they recover, then vaccinate immediately
- Ask the health worker if you are unsure

### "Are vaccines safe?"
- Yes. Vaccines used in Nigeria are approved by WHO and NAFDAC
- Vaccines go through rigorous safety testing
- The risk of the disease is far greater than any risk from the vaccine
- Millions of Nigerian children are safely vaccinated every year

### "My religion/tradition does not allow vaccination"
- All major religions in Nigeria support vaccination
- Religious leaders across Nigeria actively promote immunisation
- Vaccines save children's lives — not vaccinating puts children at serious risk

## Catch-Up Vaccination

If your child has missed any vaccines:
- Take them to the nearest health centre
- The health worker will determine which vaccines are needed
- It is never too late to start or catch up
- Your child does not need to start over — they continue from where they stopped

## Vaccine-Preventable Disease Outbreaks

Nigeria still experiences outbreaks of:
- Measles — highly contagious, can be fatal in malnourished children
- Meningitis — especially in northern Nigeria during the dry season
- Polio — Nigeria was certified polio-free in 2020, but vaccination continues to maintain this
- Yellow fever — outbreaks occur periodically

Vaccination is the best protection against all of these.

## Tips

- Keep your child's immunisation card safe — you will need it for school registration
- Do not wait for immunisation campaigns — take your child to the health centre on schedule
- If you have a new baby, start vaccination at birth
- Tell other parents about the importance of vaccination
- Immunisation is one of the most cost-effective ways to protect your child's health
