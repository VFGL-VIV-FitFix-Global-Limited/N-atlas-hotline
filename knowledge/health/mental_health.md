# Mental Health Awareness in Nigeria

## What is Mental Health?

Mental health is about your emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how you think, feel, and act. Mental health problems are real medical conditions — they are not caused by witchcraft, spiritual attacks, or personal weakness.

Mental health issues are common in Nigeria, but stigma prevents many people from seeking help. Getting help is a sign of strength, not weakness.

## Common Mental Health Conditions

### Depression
- Persistent sadness or feeling empty for weeks or months
- Loss of interest in things you used to enjoy
- Difficulty sleeping or sleeping too much
- Tiredness and lack of energy
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Thoughts of death or suicide
- Changes in appetite (eating too much or too little)

### Anxiety
- Excessive worry that is difficult to control
- Feeling restless, on edge, or unable to relax
- Racing heart, sweating, trembling
- Difficulty sleeping
- Difficulty concentrating
- Avoiding situations that cause fear

### Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- Can develop after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event (violence, accident, kidnapping, abuse)
- Flashbacks and nightmares about the event
- Avoiding places or people that remind you of the event
- Feeling emotionally numb or disconnected
- Being easily startled or constantly on alert

### Psychosis
- Hearing voices or seeing things others do not (hallucinations)
- Strong beliefs that are not based in reality (delusions)
- Confused thinking and speech
- Unusual behaviour
- This is a medical condition that responds to treatment — it is NOT spiritual possession

### Substance Abuse
- Dependence on alcohol, tramadol, codeine, cannabis, or other substances
- Inability to stop despite negative consequences
- Needing more and more to feel the effect
- Withdrawal symptoms when you stop
- Substance abuse is a health problem — not a character flaw

## When to Seek Help

- Sadness or worry lasting more than 2 weeks that affects your daily life
- Difficulty sleeping for more than 2 weeks
- Using alcohol or drugs to cope with problems
- Hearing voices or seeing things others do not
- Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life
- Sudden dramatic changes in behaviour
- Difficulty caring for yourself or your family
- Withdrawing from friends, family, and activities

## Treatment

Mental health conditions are treatable. Treatment may include:

### Counselling and Therapy
- Talking to a trained mental health professional (psychologist, counsellor, or psychiatrist)
- Helps you understand and manage your condition
- Available at hospitals and private practices
- Some organisations offer free or affordable counselling

### Medication
- Some conditions (depression, anxiety, psychosis) respond well to medication
- Medications are prescribed by doctors — do not self-medicate
- Take medication as prescribed — do not stop suddenly without consulting your doctor
- Side effects are common but usually manageable — talk to your doctor

### Lifestyle Changes
- Regular exercise improves mood
- Good sleep habits
- Eating well
- Reducing alcohol and drug use
- Staying connected with supportive people
- Engaging in activities you enjoy
- Prayer, meditation, and relaxation can complement treatment (but should not replace it)

## How to Support Someone

- Listen without judgment
- Do not say "just pray about it" or "it's not that serious"
- Encourage them to seek professional help
- Help them find a mental health professional or hospital
- Check in on them regularly
- Do not chain, lock up, or beat someone with mental illness — this is abuse and is illegal
- Be patient — recovery takes time

## Myths and Facts

**Myth:** Mental illness is caused by witchcraft or spiritual attacks.
**Fact:** Mental illness is a medical condition caused by biological, psychological, and social factors.

**Myth:** People with mental illness are violent and dangerous.
**Fact:** Most people with mental illness are not violent. They are more likely to be victims than perpetrators.

**Myth:** Mental illness cannot be treated.
**Fact:** Most mental health conditions respond well to treatment — medication, therapy, or both.

**Myth:** Only weak people get depressed.
**Fact:** Depression is a medical condition that can affect anyone, regardless of strength, status, or faith.

**Myth:** Children cannot have mental health problems.
**Fact:** Children and teenagers can experience depression, anxiety, and other conditions. Early help is important.

## Tips

- Mental health is as important as physical health
- Seeking help is brave, not weak
- If you cannot afford private care, go to a government psychiatric hospital — care is often free or very low cost
- Do not be ashamed to talk about how you feel
- Check on your friends and family — ask how they are really doing
- Reduce stigma by learning about mental health and sharing correct information
