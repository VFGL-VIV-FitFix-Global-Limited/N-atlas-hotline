# Malaria Prevention and Treatment

Malaria is one of the leading causes of illness and death in Nigeria, especially among children under 5 and pregnant women. It is caused by the Plasmodium parasite and transmitted through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

## Symptoms of Malaria

Common symptoms include:
- Fever and chills
- Headache
- Body aches and muscle pain
- Tiredness and weakness
- Nausea and vomiting
- Sweating

In children, watch for: high fever, refusal to eat, excessive crying, convulsions (fits), and drowsiness.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor immediately if you or your child has:
- High fever that does not come down with paracetamol
- Convulsions or fits
- Severe weakness or inability to stand
- Confusion or unusual drowsiness
- Dark or reduced urine
- Yellowing of the eyes

Do NOT treat severe malaria at home — go to the nearest hospital immediately.

## Prevention

1. Sleep under insecticide-treated mosquito nets (ITNs) every night — especially children and pregnant women.
2. Use mosquito repellent sprays or coils in the evening.
3. Clear stagnant water around your home — this is where mosquitoes breed.
4. Wear long sleeves and trousers in the evening.
5. Install window and door screens.
6. Pregnant women should attend antenatal clinics for Intermittent Preventive Treatment (IPT).

## Treatment

- Visit a health facility for a rapid diagnostic test (RDT) before taking any medication.
- The recommended treatment is Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT).
- Complete the full course of medication even if you feel better.
- Paracetamol can be used for fever while waiting to see a doctor.
