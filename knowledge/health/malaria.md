# Malaria Prevention and Treatment in Nigeria

## What is Malaria?

Malaria is a serious disease caused by parasites spread through the bite of infected mosquitoes. It is the most common cause of illness and death in Nigeria, especially among children under 5 and pregnant women.

## Symptoms

- High fever and chills
- Headache
- Body pain and weakness
- Sweating
- Loss of appetite
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhoea (sometimes)

In severe cases:
- Convulsions (especially in children)
- Confusion or unconsciousness
- Dark-coloured urine
- Severe anaemia
- Difficulty breathing

## Prevention

### Use Insecticide-Treated Nets (ITNs)
- Sleep under a treated mosquito net every night
- Free nets are distributed during government campaigns at health centres
- Hang the net so it covers the entire bed and tuck it under the mattress
- Treat or replace nets every 3 years

### Remove Mosquito Breeding Sites
- Clear stagnant water around your home (old tyres, blocked gutters, open containers)
- Cover water storage containers
- Cut grass and bush around your compound

### Use Insect Repellent and Protective Clothing
- Wear long sleeves and trousers in the evening
- Use mosquito repellent on exposed skin
- Use window and door screens if possible

### Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS)
- Allow government health workers to spray the inside of your home when they come around

## Treatment

### Get Tested First
- Do not just treat malaria without a test — other illnesses have similar symptoms
- Visit a health centre or pharmacy for a Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) or blood test
- RDTs are quick and often free at government health centres

### Recommended Treatment
- The recommended treatment in Nigeria is **ACT (Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy)**
- Common ACTs include: Coartem (artemether-lumefantrine), Artesunate-Amodiaquine
- Take the full course even if you feel better after 1-2 days
- Do not use chloroquine alone — malaria parasites in Nigeria are resistant to it

### For Severe Malaria
- Go to a hospital immediately
- Severe malaria requires injectable artesunate and hospital admission
- Do not delay — severe malaria can kill within hours, especially in children

## Malaria in Children

- Children under 5 are most at risk of dying from malaria
- If your child has fever, take them for a malaria test immediately
- Give the child plenty of fluids
- Use tepid sponging (lukewarm water) to bring down high fever — do not use cold water or ice
- Give paracetamol for fever (correct dose for age/weight)
- Do not give aspirin to children

## Malaria in Pregnancy

- Pregnant women are at high risk for severe malaria
- Attend antenatal care (ANC) — you will receive:
  - IPTp (Intermittent Preventive Treatment in Pregnancy) with Sulfadoxine-Pyrimethamine (SP/Fansidar)
  - Free insecticide-treated nets
- Take all doses of IPTp as prescribed during ANC visits
- If you develop fever during pregnancy, go to the hospital immediately — do not self-medicate

## When to Go to the Hospital

- Fever lasting more than 2 days despite treatment
- Vomiting so much you cannot keep medicine down
- Convulsions
- Confusion or unconsciousness
- Very pale palms or eyes (severe anaemia)
- Dark or reduced urine
- Difficulty breathing
- Any severe symptoms in a child under 5 or a pregnant woman

## Cost of Treatment

- Malaria testing is often free at government primary health centres
- ACTs are subsidised and may cost ₦500 to ₦2,000 at pharmacies
- Government health centres may provide free treatment for children under 5 and pregnant women

## Tips

- Do not rely on herbal mixtures (agbo) alone for malaria treatment
- Always complete the full course of ACT medication
- Keep your environment clean to reduce mosquitoes
- If you live in a malaria-prone area, prevention is cheaper than treatment
