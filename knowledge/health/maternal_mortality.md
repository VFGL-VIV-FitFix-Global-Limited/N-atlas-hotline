# Maternal Health and Safe Delivery in Nigeria

## Why This Matters

Nigeria has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in the world. Thousands of women die every year from pregnancy-related complications that are preventable. The five main killers are: excessive bleeding after delivery, infections, high blood pressure (eclampsia), unsafe abortion, and obstructed labour.

## The Five Main Killers

### 1. Excessive Bleeding (Postpartum Haemorrhage)
- Heavy bleeding after delivery is the number one killer
- Warning signs: soaking through pads very quickly, feeling dizzy or faint, pale skin
- What to do: Go to the hospital immediately — this is a life-threatening emergency
- Prevention: Deliver in a health facility where drugs and blood transfusion are available

### 2. Infections (Sepsis)
- Infection after delivery, often from unclean delivery conditions
- Warning signs: fever, foul-smelling discharge, abdominal pain after delivery
- What to do: See a doctor immediately — antibiotics are needed
- Prevention: Deliver in a clean health facility with trained staff

### 3. High Blood Pressure and Eclampsia
- Dangerously high blood pressure during pregnancy or after delivery
- Warning signs: severe headache, blurred vision, swelling of face and hands, convulsions
- What to do: This is a medical emergency — go to the hospital immediately
- Prevention: Attend antenatal care — blood pressure is checked every visit

### 4. Unsafe Abortion
- Attempting to end a pregnancy using unsafe methods or unqualified persons
- Can cause heavy bleeding, infection, organ damage, and death
- What to do: If you are bleeding heavily after an attempted abortion, go to the hospital — you will be treated, not punished
- Prevention: Family planning prevents unintended pregnancies

### 5. Obstructed Labour
- When the baby cannot pass through the birth canal
- Warning signs: labour lasting more than 12 hours, severe pain, no progress
- What to do: Go to a hospital — a caesarean section may be needed
- Prevention: Deliver in a health facility where emergency surgery is available

## How to Protect Yourself

### Attend Antenatal Care
- Start ANC in the first 3 months of pregnancy
- Attend all scheduled visits
- Follow your doctor or midwife's advice

### Deliver in a Health Facility
- Deliver at a hospital, health centre, or registered maternity centre
- Have a skilled birth attendant (doctor, midwife, or nurse)
- Home delivery is risky — complications can happen suddenly

### Have a Birth Preparedness Plan
- Know which health facility you will deliver at
- Arrange transport in advance (not everyone has a car — plan with a neighbour, taxi, or keke)
- Save money for delivery costs and possible emergencies
- Identify at least 2 potential blood donors with your blood type
- Pack a delivery bag early

### Use Family Planning
- Space your pregnancies by at least 2 years
- Family planning methods are available free at government health centres
- Fewer, well-spaced pregnancies are safer for both mother and children
- Talk to your health worker about the best method for you

### Nutrition and Rest
- Eat well during pregnancy (balanced diet with protein, vegetables, iron-rich foods)
- Take iron and folic acid tablets as prescribed
- Get enough rest
- Avoid heavy lifting

## Danger Signs — Go to Hospital Immediately

During pregnancy:
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Severe headache with blurred vision
- High fever
- Severe abdominal pain
- Water breaking before 37 weeks
- Baby not moving for more than 12 hours
- Swelling of face and hands
- Convulsions

During or after delivery:
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop
- Fever
- Foul-smelling discharge
- Severe headache or convulsions
- Difficulty breathing

## For Men and Family Members

- Support the pregnant woman to attend all ANC visits
- Save money for delivery and emergency costs
- Know the danger signs and be ready to act
- Arrange transport to the hospital
- Be ready to donate blood if needed
- Support her decision to deliver in a health facility
- Do not delay going to the hospital if there are danger signs

## Free and Subsidised Services

Many states in Nigeria offer free or subsidised maternal health services:
- Free antenatal care at government primary health centres
- Free delivery at some government hospitals (check your state)
- Free caesarean sections in some states
- Free family planning services at most government health centres

Ask your Local Government Health Department about what is available in your area.

## Tips

- Most maternal deaths are preventable with proper care and quick action
- Do not rely solely on traditional birth attendants (TBAs) for delivery — they cannot handle emergencies
- Time is critical — delays in reaching a hospital cost lives
- Blood donation saves lives — know your blood type and be willing to donate
