# Pregnancy and Antenatal Care

Every pregnant woman in Nigeria should register at a health facility as soon as she knows she is pregnant. Antenatal care helps prevent problems for mother and baby.

## What to Expect at Antenatal Visits

- Blood pressure, weight and urine checks
- Tests for malaria, HIV, hepatitis and anaemia
- Tetanus vaccination
- Iron and folic acid tablets
- Intermittent Preventive Treatment (IPT) for malaria
- Advice on diet, rest and danger signs

The World Health Organization recommends at least eight contacts during pregnancy. Start in the first three months if possible.

## Danger Signs — Go to Hospital Immediately

- Heavy bleeding
- Severe headache or blurred vision
- Convulsions
- Severe abdominal pain
- Reduced or no baby movements
- High fever
- Sudden swelling of face, hands or feet

## Delivery

Plan to deliver in a health facility with a skilled birth attendant. Prepare transport money and a delivery bag (clean cloths, soap, baby clothes, your antenatal card).

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis. Visit your nearest primary health centre or hospital. For emergencies, call 112.
