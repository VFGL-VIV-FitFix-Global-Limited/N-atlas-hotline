# Typhoid Fever in Nigeria

## What is Typhoid?

Typhoid fever is a bacterial infection caused by Salmonella typhi. It spreads through contaminated food and water. Typhoid is very common in Nigeria, especially in areas with poor water supply and sanitation.

## Symptoms

Typhoid symptoms usually develop 1-3 weeks after infection:

- High fever that increases gradually day by day (can reach 39-40°C)
- Headache
- Body weakness and fatigue
- Stomach pain
- Loss of appetite
- Constipation (more common in adults) or diarrhoea (more common in children)
- Coated tongue (white or brownish coating)
- Rash on the chest or abdomen (rose-coloured spots — not always present)

In severe or untreated cases:
- Intestinal bleeding (blood in stool)
- Intestinal perforation (hole in the intestine — a surgical emergency)
- Confusion or delirium
- Severe weakness

## Diagnosis

- **Widal test** — commonly used in Nigeria, but not always accurate. A single Widal test can give false results.
- **Blood culture** — the most accurate test. Your doctor takes a blood sample and grows it in the lab.
- **Stool and urine culture** — also used for diagnosis
- Tell your doctor all your symptoms and how long you have had fever

**Important:** Many Nigerians self-diagnose typhoid based on symptoms alone. Always get a proper test before treatment — other illnesses (malaria, urinary infections) have similar symptoms.

## Treatment

- Typhoid is treated with **antibiotics** prescribed by a doctor
- Common antibiotics used: ciprofloxacin, azithromycin, ceftriaxone
- Complete the full course of antibiotics even if you feel better — stopping early can cause the infection to return or become resistant
- Drink plenty of fluids
- Rest
- Eat soft, easily digestible foods

**Do not self-medicate.** Antibiotic resistance is a growing problem in Nigeria. Using the wrong antibiotic or not completing the course makes resistance worse.

## Prevention

### Safe Water
- Drink only boiled or treated water
- Use water purification tablets if boiling is not possible
- Do not drink water from untreated wells, streams, or rivers
- Store drinking water in clean, covered containers

### Food Hygiene
- Wash hands with soap before preparing or eating food
- Cook food thoroughly — especially meat, eggs, and seafood
- Eat food while it is hot
- Wash fruits and vegetables with clean water before eating
- Be careful with food from roadside vendors — ensure it is freshly prepared and hot

### Personal Hygiene
- Wash hands with soap after using the toilet
- Use a clean toilet or latrine
- Do not prepare food for others if you have typhoid

### Vaccination
- Typhoid vaccine is available in Nigeria
- Recommended for travellers and people in high-risk areas
- Available at private clinics and some government hospitals
- The vaccine reduces risk but does not guarantee 100% protection — hygiene is still essential

## When to Go to the Hospital

- Fever lasting more than 5 days
- Fever not responding to paracetamol
- Blood in stool
- Severe stomach pain
- Inability to eat or drink
- Confusion or extreme weakness
- If you have been diagnosed and symptoms worsen despite treatment

## Typhoid Carriers

Some people recover from typhoid but continue to carry the bacteria and can spread it to others through food and water, even though they feel fine. If you have had typhoid:
- Get a follow-up stool test to confirm you are no longer carrying the bacteria
- Practise strict hand hygiene, especially if you handle food

## Tips

- Do not assume every fever is typhoid — get tested
- Do not buy antibiotics from the market without a prescription
- Completing your antibiotics is crucial — partial treatment leads to drug resistance
- Typhoid often comes back if not properly treated
- If multiple family members have typhoid, check your water source — it may be contaminated
