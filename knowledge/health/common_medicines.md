# Common Medicines and Safe Use in Nigeria

## Important Warning

This guide provides general information only. Always consult a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine. Do not self-medicate for serious conditions.

## Paracetamol (Panadol, Emzor Paracetamol)

**What it treats:** Fever, headache, body pain, toothache, menstrual pain
**Adult dose:** 500mg-1000mg (1-2 tablets) every 4-6 hours, maximum 4000mg (8 tablets) in 24 hours
**Children:** Use paracetamol syrup — dose based on age and weight (check the bottle or ask a pharmacist)
**Warning:**
- Do not exceed the maximum dose — overdose damages the liver
- Do not take with alcohol
- Do not give aspirin to children — use paracetamol instead

## Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)

**What it treats:** Dehydration from diarrhoea, vomiting, or excessive sweating
**How to use:** Dissolve one sachet in 1 litre of clean water, sip frequently
**Available:** At pharmacies, chemists, and health centres — very cheap (₦50-₦200 per sachet)
**Note:** ORS does not stop diarrhoea — it replaces lost fluids to prevent dangerous dehydration

## Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)

Examples: Coartem (artemether-lumefantrine), Artesunate-Amodiaquine, Lonart

**What it treats:** Malaria (confirmed by test)
**How to use:** Follow the dosage on the pack — usually taken twice daily for 3 days
**Warning:**
- Only take for confirmed malaria (after a test)
- Complete the full 3-day course even if you feel better
- Do not use chloroquine alone — malaria in Nigeria is resistant to it
- Do not take ACT as "prevention" — it is for treatment only

## Ibuprofen (Brufen, Ibugesic)

**What it treats:** Pain, inflammation, fever, menstrual cramps, headache, muscle pain
**Adult dose:** 200mg-400mg every 4-6 hours, maximum 1200mg in 24 hours (over-the-counter)
**Warning:**
- Take with food — can cause stomach ulcers if taken on an empty stomach
- Do not take if you have stomach ulcers, kidney problems, or are pregnant
- Not recommended for children under 6 months
- Do not combine with aspirin

## Antacids (Gestid, Maalox, Gaviscon)

**What they treat:** Heartburn, stomach acid, indigestion
**How to use:** Chew or take liquid as directed on the package, usually after meals
**Note:** If heartburn is frequent, see a doctor — it could indicate a more serious condition

## Metformin

**What it treats:** Type 2 diabetes
**How to use:** Only as prescribed by a doctor
**Warning:**
- Do not stop taking without doctor's advice
- Take with food to reduce stomach upset
- This is a lifelong medication — do not substitute with herbal mixtures

## Amlodipine

**What it treats:** High blood pressure
**How to use:** Only as prescribed by a doctor, usually once daily
**Warning:**
- Take every day, even if you feel fine
- Do not stop suddenly
- May cause ankle swelling — report to your doctor

## Amoxicillin

**What it treats:** Bacterial infections (chest infection, ear infection, urinary infection, skin infection)
**How to use:** As prescribed by a doctor, usually 3 times daily for 5-7 days
**Warning:**
- Complete the full course — stopping early creates drug-resistant bacteria
- Tell your doctor if you are allergic to penicillin
- Do not use antibiotics for viral infections (cold, flu) — they will not work

## Safe Medicine Practices

### Do
- Buy medicines from registered pharmacies and licensed chemists
- Check the expiry date before buying or taking any medicine
- Read the label and follow dosage instructions
- Complete the full course of antibiotics
- Store medicines in a cool, dry place, away from children
- Tell your doctor about all medicines you are taking (including herbal ones)
- Ask your pharmacist if you are unsure about anything

### Do Not
- Do not buy medicines from street vendors, bus hawkers, or unlicensed sellers
- Do not share prescription medicines with others
- Do not take medicines prescribed for someone else
- Do not use expired medicines
- Do not take herbal mixtures alongside prescribed medicines without asking your doctor — interactions can be dangerous
- Do not give adult medicines to children
- Do not increase the dose because you want to "feel better faster"
- Do not mix antibiotics without a doctor's guidance

## Fake and Substandard Medicines

Fake medicines are a serious problem in Nigeria. They may contain wrong ingredients, wrong amounts, or no active ingredient at all.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Buy from registered pharmacies only
- Check NAFDAC registration number on the packaging
- Use NAFDAC's Mobile Authentication Service (MAS): Scratch the panel on the medicine, text the code to 38353 to verify
- Report suspected fake medicines to NAFDAC

## Tips

- Your pharmacist is a valuable resource — ask them questions
- Keep a basic medicine kit at home: paracetamol, ORS, antiseptic, bandages, thermometer
- Never use tramadol, codeine, or other controlled substances without a prescription — they are addictive and dangerous
- Traditional/herbal medicines are not regulated — their safety and effectiveness are not guaranteed
