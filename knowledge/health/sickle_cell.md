# Sickle Cell Disease in Nigeria

## What is Sickle Cell Disease?

Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) is an inherited blood disorder where red blood cells become sickle-shaped (like a crescent moon) instead of round. These sickle-shaped cells get stuck in blood vessels, blocking blood flow and causing pain, organ damage, and other serious complications.

Nigeria has the highest number of sickle cell births in the world — about 150,000 babies are born with SCD every year in Nigeria.

## Genotypes and Risk

Every person has a haemoglobin genotype inherited from both parents:

| Your Genotype | What It Means |
|---|---|
| AA | Normal — no sickle cell gene |
| AS | Sickle cell trait (carrier) — usually healthy but carries one sickle gene |
| SS | Sickle cell disease — has the condition |
| SC | Another form of sickle cell disease (milder than SS but still serious) |
| AC | Carrier of haemoglobin C — usually healthy |

### Marriage and Genotype Compatibility

| Partner 1 | Partner 2 | Risk to Children |
|---|---|---|
| AA | AA | All children AA — safe |
| AA | AS | Children will be AA or AS — safe (no SS) |
| AA | SS | All children AS (carriers) — safe |
| AS | AS | 25% chance of SS child in each pregnancy — risky |
| AS | SS | 50% chance of SS, 50% AS — very risky |
| SS | SS | All children SS — very risky |

**Know your genotype before marriage.** If both partners are AS, there is a 1 in 4 chance that each child will have sickle cell disease (SS).

## Symptoms

Symptoms usually begin from 6 months of age:
- Pain crises: severe pain in bones, joints, chest, abdomen — this is the most common symptom
- Frequent infections (especially pneumonia and malaria)
- Anaemia: tiredness, weakness, pale palms and eyes
- Swelling of hands and feet (dactylitis) — often the first sign in babies
- Delayed growth in children
- Jaundice (yellowish eyes)
- Bed-wetting (in older children)

### Serious Complications
- Stroke (can occur even in children)
- Acute chest syndrome (fever, chest pain, difficulty breathing)
- Splenic sequestration (sudden severe anaemia from blood pooling in the spleen)
- Organ damage (kidneys, liver, lungs)
- Leg ulcers
- Priapism (painful prolonged erection in males)
- Avascular necrosis (bone damage, especially in the hip)

## Management

There is no widely available cure for sickle cell disease in Nigeria, but proper management can prevent complications and improve quality of life.

### Daily Management
- Drink plenty of water every day (at least 2-3 litres) — dehydration triggers crises
- Eat nutritious, balanced meals
- Take folic acid daily (as prescribed)
- Take prescribed medications regularly
- Avoid extreme cold or extreme heat
- Avoid strenuous exercise — moderate activity is fine
- Get enough rest and sleep
- Avoid high altitudes
- Wear warm clothing in cold weather

### Preventing Pain Crises
- Stay well hydrated
- Avoid cold temperatures (cold water baths, cold drinks in excess, air conditioning set too low)
- Avoid infections — wash hands regularly, eat clean food
- Take malaria prevention seriously — sleep under treated nets, get tested and treated promptly
- Avoid stress and overexertion
- Take prescribed preventive medications (hydroxyurea, penicillin)

### Medications
- **Folic acid:** Daily supplement to help produce red blood cells
- **Hydroxyurea:** Reduces frequency and severity of pain crises — prescribed by a specialist
- **Penicillin V:** Daily antibiotic for children to prevent infections
- **Pain relief:** Paracetamol and ibuprofen for mild pain; stronger painkillers for severe crises (prescribed by doctor)
- **Malaria prevention:** Sleep under insecticide-treated nets; prompt treatment of any fever

### Immunisations
Children with SCD should receive all routine immunisations PLUS:
- Pneumococcal vaccine
- Meningococcal vaccine
- Annual flu vaccine (if available)

### Regular Check-ups
- Attend a sickle cell clinic regularly
- Blood tests every 3-6 months
- Eye checks annually
- Check for organ damage as recommended by your doctor

## When to Go to the Hospital

Go immediately if you or your child has:
- Severe pain that does not respond to home painkillers
- Fever (any fever in a child with SCD is an emergency)
- Sudden severe anaemia (very pale, very weak, rapid breathing)
- Swollen, hard abdomen
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body (possible stroke)
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Prolonged painful erection
- Sudden vision problems
- Seizures

## Where to Get Help

- Sickle cell clinics at teaching hospitals and general hospitals
- Federal Medical Centre sickle cell units
- Sickle Cell Foundation Nigeria: sicklecellfoundation.com
- State sickle cell centres (available in several states)

## Tips

- Know your genotype — test before marriage
- Know your children's genotype — test at birth or early infancy
- People with sickle cell disease can live full, productive lives with proper management
- Do not stigmatise people with SCD — it is not contagious or a curse
- Join a sickle cell support group for emotional support and practical advice
- Children with SCD can attend school — inform the school about their condition
- Bone marrow transplant can cure SCD in some cases, but it is expensive and not widely available in Nigeria
