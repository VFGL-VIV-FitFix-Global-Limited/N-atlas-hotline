# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension) in Nigeria

## What is Hypertension?

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is when the force of blood pushing against your blood vessel walls is consistently too high. It is called the "silent killer" because most people with hypertension do not feel any symptoms until serious damage has occurred to the heart, brain, kidneys, or eyes.

Hypertension is extremely common in Nigeria — estimates suggest 1 in 3 Nigerian adults has it, and many do not know.

## Understanding Blood Pressure Numbers

Blood pressure is measured as two numbers (e.g., 120/80 mmHg):
- **Top number (systolic):** Pressure when the heart beats
- **Bottom number (diastolic):** Pressure when the heart rests between beats

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Below 120 | Below 80 |
| Elevated | 120-129 | Below 80 |
| Stage 1 Hypertension | 130-139 | 80-89 |
| Stage 2 Hypertension | 140 or higher | 90 or higher |
| Crisis (go to hospital) | Above 180 | Above 120 |

## Symptoms

Most people have NO symptoms. That is why regular checking is important.

When symptoms appear, the condition may already be severe:
- Headache (especially at the back of the head, in the morning)
- Dizziness
- Blurred vision
- Nosebleeds
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Palpitations (heart beating fast or irregularly)

## Risk Factors

- Age (risk increases after 40, but young people can have it too)
- Family history of hypertension
- Excessive salt intake
- Obesity or overweight
- Lack of physical activity
- Excessive alcohol intake
- Smoking
- Stress
- Diabetes
- Kidney disease

## How to Check

- Visit any hospital, health centre, pharmacy, or health screening event
- Blood pressure checks are quick, painless, and usually free or very cheap
- You can buy a home blood pressure monitor at a pharmacy (₦10,000-₦30,000)
- Check at least once every year if you are over 30
- If you have been diagnosed, check regularly as your doctor advises

## Management

### Lifestyle Changes

These are essential whether or not you take medication:

1. **Reduce Salt**
   - Use less salt in cooking — taste food before adding salt
   - Avoid seasoning cubes in excess (Maggi, Knorr — they are very high in sodium)
   - Avoid processed and packaged foods
   - Limit smoked fish, stockfish (okporoko), and dried crayfish (they have high salt content)

2. **Eat Healthy**
   - Eat more fruits and vegetables
   - Eat more potassium-rich foods: bananas, potatoes, beans, spinach
   - Choose lean proteins: fish, beans, chicken without skin
   - Reduce fried foods — use less oil in cooking
   - Choose whole grains over refined carbohydrates

3. **Exercise Regularly**
   - At least 30 minutes of moderate exercise on most days
   - Walking, cycling, swimming, dancing
   - Start slowly if you have not been active

4. **Maintain a Healthy Weight**
   - Losing even 5-10 kg can significantly lower blood pressure
   - Ask your doctor what a healthy weight is for your height

5. **Limit Alcohol**
   - If you drink, limit to no more than 1-2 drinks per day
   - Excessive alcohol raises blood pressure

6. **Stop Smoking**
   - Smoking damages blood vessels and raises blood pressure
   - Seek help to quit if needed

7. **Manage Stress**
   - Find healthy ways to manage stress: prayer, exercise, talking to friends and family, rest
   - Avoid overworking without rest

### Medication

- If your doctor prescribes blood pressure medication, take it every day as directed
- Common medications: Amlodipine, Lisinopril, Losartan, Hydrochlorothiazide, Atenolol
- Do NOT stop taking medication because you feel fine — hypertension is usually lifelong
- Do not skip doses or share medication with others
- Report any side effects to your doctor — they can adjust your medication
- Medication costs vary but many are available at ₦500-₦3,000 per month

## Complications

Untreated hypertension can cause:
- Stroke (the leading cause of stroke in Nigeria)
- Heart attack and heart failure
- Kidney failure
- Vision loss
- Sexual dysfunction
- Dementia
- Peripheral artery disease

## Hypertension in Pregnancy (Pre-eclampsia)

- High blood pressure during pregnancy is called pre-eclampsia
- Symptoms: high blood pressure, protein in urine, swelling of face and hands, headache, blurred vision
- Pre-eclampsia is dangerous for both mother and baby
- Attend all antenatal appointments — blood pressure is checked every visit
- If you have pre-eclampsia, your doctor may recommend early delivery
- Eclampsia (convulsions from severe pre-eclampsia) is a medical emergency — go to the hospital immediately

## When to Go to the Hospital

- Blood pressure above 180/120 (hypertensive crisis)
- Severe headache with nausea or vomiting
- Chest pain
- Difficulty breathing
- Vision changes or sudden vision loss
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body (possible stroke)
- Confusion or difficulty speaking

## Tips

- Check your blood pressure regularly — do not wait for symptoms
- Take your medication every day, even when you feel fine
- Do not rely on herbal remedies to control blood pressure
- Reduce seasoning cubes and excessive salt in your cooking
- Walk for at least 30 minutes daily
- Teach your family members about hypertension — it runs in families
