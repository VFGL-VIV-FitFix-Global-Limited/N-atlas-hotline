# Basic First Aid Guide

## Burns

### Minor Burns (small area, no blistering or mild blistering)
1. Cool the burn immediately under clean, cool running water for at least 10 minutes
2. Do NOT use ice, butter, toothpaste, or palm oil on the burn
3. Cover with a clean, dry bandage or cloth
4. Take paracetamol for pain
5. Do not burst blisters — they protect the skin underneath

### Severe Burns (large area, deep burns, face/hands/genitals, or electrical/chemical burns)
1. Call for help immediately — go to the nearest hospital
2. Cool with clean water if possible
3. Do NOT remove clothing stuck to the burn
4. Cover loosely with a clean cloth
5. Do not apply any substance
6. Keep the person warm and give sips of water if they are conscious

## Cuts and Wounds

### Minor Cuts
1. Wash your hands with soap
2. Clean the wound gently with clean water
3. Apply pressure with a clean cloth to stop bleeding
4. Apply antiseptic (iodine or hydrogen peroxide)
5. Cover with a clean bandage or plaster
6. Change the dressing daily

### Deep or Serious Wounds
1. Apply firm pressure with a clean cloth to stop bleeding
2. If blood soaks through, add another cloth on top — do not remove the first one
3. Elevate the injured area above the heart if possible
4. Go to the hospital — the wound may need stitches
5. If the wound was caused by a rusty or dirty object, you may need a tetanus injection

## Choking

### Adult or Child (over 1 year)
1. Encourage the person to cough — this is the best way to clear a blockage
2. If coughing does not work, give 5 back blows: lean the person forward, hit firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand
3. If back blows do not work, give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre): stand behind the person, place your fist just above their navel, grasp with your other hand, and pull sharply inward and upward
4. Alternate between 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts
5. If the person becomes unconscious, lower them to the ground and call for help

### Baby (under 1 year)
1. Lay the baby face-down along your forearm, supporting the head
2. Give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades
3. Turn the baby face-up and give 5 chest thrusts (press firmly on the breastbone with 2 fingers)
4. Check the mouth for any visible object — remove it carefully if you can see it. Do not sweep blindly.
5. Repeat until the object is cleared or help arrives

## Snake Bite

1. Keep the person calm and still — movement spreads venom faster
2. Remove rings, watches, or tight clothing near the bite before swelling starts
3. Immobilise the bitten limb (keep it still, as if it were broken)
4. Get to the nearest hospital as quickly as possible for anti-venom
5. Try to remember what the snake looked like (colour, size, head shape) — this helps the doctor choose the right treatment

**Do NOT:**
- Do not cut the bite wound
- Do not suck out the venom
- Do not tie a tight tourniquet
- Do not apply a black stone, herbs, or chemicals
- Do not give alcohol

## Drowning

1. Get the person out of the water safely — do not put yourself at risk
2. Call for help
3. If the person is not breathing, start CPR immediately:
   - Place the heel of your hand on the centre of the chest
   - Press down hard and fast (about 5cm deep, 100-120 times per minute)
   - Give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions (tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose, blow into the mouth)
4. Continue CPR until the person breathes or help arrives
5. If the person is breathing, place them on their side (recovery position)
6. Get them to the hospital even if they seem fine — delayed drowning complications can occur

## Fractures (Broken Bones)

1. Do not try to straighten the broken limb
2. Immobilise the area — use a splint (a stick, ruler, or rolled-up newspaper) and tie with cloth above and below the break
3. Apply ice wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling (do not apply ice directly to skin)
4. Go to the hospital for an X-ray
5. Give paracetamol for pain — do not give aspirin

## Electric Shock

1. Do NOT touch the person while they are still in contact with the electrical source
2. Turn off the power source if possible, or use a dry non-conducting object (wooden stick, plastic chair, dry rubber) to move the person away from the source
3. Call for help
4. If the person is not breathing, start CPR
5. Treat any burns with cool water
6. Go to the hospital — even if the person seems fine, internal injuries can occur

## Heat Stroke

Common during Nigeria's hot season:
1. Move the person to a shady or cool area
2. Remove excess clothing
3. Cool the person: fan them, apply wet cloths to neck, armpits, and groin
4. Give small sips of cool water if the person is conscious
5. Go to the hospital if the person is confused, unconscious, or not improving

## When to Call for Emergency Help

Call 112 (National Emergency Number) or go to the nearest hospital if:
- The person is unconscious or not breathing
- There is heavy bleeding that does not stop with pressure
- There is a suspected spinal or head injury
- The person has been bitten by a snake
- There are signs of heart attack: chest pain, pain in left arm, difficulty breathing
- There are signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech
- Burns covering a large area of the body
- Poisoning or suspected poisoning

## Tips

- Keep a basic first aid kit at home: bandages, antiseptic, paracetamol, ORS, cotton wool, plasters, scissors, thermometer
- Learn basic CPR — it can save a life
- Do not move someone with a suspected spinal injury unless they are in immediate danger
- Stay calm in emergencies — panic makes things worse
- Know the location of the nearest hospital or health centre
