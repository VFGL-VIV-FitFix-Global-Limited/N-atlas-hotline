# Nutrition and Healthy Eating in Nigeria

## Why Good Nutrition Matters

Good nutrition keeps you healthy, gives you energy, prevents diseases, and helps children grow properly. Many health problems in Nigeria — malnutrition, stunting in children, obesity, diabetes, hypertension — are linked to poor diet.

## Balanced Diet

A balanced meal should contain:

### 1. Energy Foods (Carbohydrates)
- Rice, yam, cassava (garri, fufu, eba), plantain, potatoes, bread, maize (corn), millet, sorghum, guinea corn
- These give your body energy
- Eat moderate portions — too much leads to weight gain

### 2. Body-Building Foods (Proteins)
- Beans, lentils, groundnuts, soya beans
- Fish, chicken, meat, eggs
- Milk, yoghurt, cheese
- Crayfish, snails, mushrooms
- These build and repair muscles, bones, and tissues

### 3. Protective Foods (Vitamins and Minerals)
- Vegetables: spinach (efo tete), ugu (fluted pumpkin leaves), ewedu, waterleaf, bitter leaf, okra, tomatoes, peppers, onions, garden egg, cabbage, carrots
- Fruits: oranges, bananas, pawpaw (papaya), mangoes, watermelon, pineapple, guava, apples
- These protect against diseases and keep your body functioning properly

### 4. Fats and Oils (in moderation)
- Palm oil, groundnut oil, soya bean oil, olive oil
- Nuts and seeds
- Avocado
- Use small amounts — too much fat causes weight gain and heart disease

### 5. Water
- Drink at least 6-8 cups of clean water daily
- More in hot weather or when working hard
- Water keeps the body hydrated and helps all organs function

## Nutrition for Children

### Breastfeeding (0-6 months)
- Exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months — no water, no formula, no pap
- Breast milk contains everything the baby needs
- Start breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth
- Breastfeed on demand (whenever the baby is hungry)

### Complementary Feeding (6-24 months)
- At 6 months, start introducing soft foods alongside breast milk
- Start with smooth pap (ogi) enriched with groundnut paste, soya bean flour, crayfish, or mashed beans
- Gradually add mashed vegetables, fruits, eggs, fish, and meat
- Feed the child at least 3-5 times daily in addition to breast milk
- Continue breastfeeding until at least 2 years

### Signs of Malnutrition in Children
- Underweight or very thin
- Swelling of feet, hands, or face (kwashiorkor)
- Hair changing colour (reddish or yellowish)
- Frequent illness
- Poor growth — not gaining weight or height
- Lack of energy, always tired or crying

If you notice these signs, take the child to the nearest health centre immediately. Free treatment for severe malnutrition is available at many government health facilities.

## Common Nutritional Problems in Nigeria

### Iron Deficiency (Anaemia)
- Very common in women and children
- Symptoms: tiredness, weakness, pale palms and eyes, dizziness
- Eat iron-rich foods: dark leafy vegetables, beans, meat, liver, eggs
- Eat vitamin C foods (oranges, tomatoes) with iron-rich foods to help absorption
- Take iron supplements if prescribed

### Vitamin A Deficiency
- Common in children — can cause blindness and increased infections
- Eat vitamin A-rich foods: palm oil, carrots, sweet potatoes, mangoes, dark green vegetables, eggs, liver
- Children receive free Vitamin A supplements during immunisation campaigns

### Iodine Deficiency
- Can cause goitre (swelling in the neck) and developmental problems in children
- Use iodised salt for cooking — check the label
- Most salt sold in Nigeria is iodised by law

## Healthy Eating Tips for Adults

### What to Eat More Of
- Vegetables and fruits — eat some with every meal
- Beans, lentils, and other legumes — cheap and nutritious
- Fish — at least twice a week
- Whole grains where possible

### What to Reduce
- Sugar: reduce soft drinks (Coke, Fanta, Malt), sugary snacks, and adding sugar to pap or beverages
- Salt: reduce seasoning cubes (Maggi, Knorr), table salt, and salty snacks
- Fried foods: reduce fried meat, fried plantain, fried yam — try boiling, grilling, or steaming instead
- Processed foods: reduce instant noodles, canned foods, sausages
- Alcohol

### Eating on a Budget
- Beans are one of the cheapest and most nutritious foods
- Buy vegetables and fruits in season — they are cheaper
- Grow vegetables at home if you have space (ugu, spinach, tomatoes)
- Buy staples in bulk from markets
- Cook at home instead of buying food outside — it is usually healthier and cheaper

## Tips

- A colourful plate is usually a healthy plate — add different coloured vegetables
- Do not skip breakfast — it gives you energy for the day
- Drink water instead of soft drinks
- Do not over-rely on one type of food (e.g., only garri or only rice)
- Wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly before eating
- Always use clean water for cooking and drinking
