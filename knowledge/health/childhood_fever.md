# Managing Childhood Fever in Nigeria

## What is Fever?

Fever is when a child's body temperature is higher than normal (above 37.5°C or 99.5°F). Fever itself is not a disease — it is a sign that the body is fighting an infection. However, high or prolonged fever in children can be dangerous and needs attention.

## Common Causes of Fever in Children

- Malaria (most common in Nigeria)
- Typhoid fever
- Upper respiratory infections (cold, cough, sore throat)
- Ear infections
- Urinary tract infections
- Measles, chickenpox, and other childhood infections
- Teething (mild fever only)
- Diarrhoea and vomiting from infections

## When to Worry

Take your child to a health facility immediately if:
- The child is under 3 months old with any fever
- Fever is above 39°C (102.2°F)
- Fever has lasted more than 2 days
- The child is not eating or drinking
- The child is very sleepy or difficult to wake
- The child has convulsions (fits/seizures)
- The child has a stiff neck
- The child has a rash
- The child is breathing fast or with difficulty
- The child has not urinated for more than 6 hours
- The child looks very pale or very weak

## What to Do at Home

### Bring Down the Fever
- Give paracetamol (not aspirin) at the correct dose for the child's age and weight
- Use tepid sponging: use a cloth soaked in lukewarm water to wipe the child's body — do NOT use cold water, ice, or alcohol
- Dress the child in light clothing — do not over-wrap
- Keep the room well-ventilated

### Keep the Child Hydrated
- Give plenty of fluids: breast milk (for breastfeeding babies), clean water, ORS (oral rehydration solution)
- Offer small sips frequently if the child is vomiting
- Continue breastfeeding

### Monitor the Child
- Check the temperature regularly (use a thermometer if you have one)
- Watch for danger signs listed above
- Note when the fever started and any other symptoms

## What NOT to Do

- Do not give aspirin to children — it can cause a dangerous condition called Reye's syndrome
- Do not use cold water or ice baths — this can cause shivering which raises the temperature further
- Do not rub methylated spirit or alcohol on the child's skin
- Do not over-dose on paracetamol — follow the dosage instructions
- Do not give adult medicine to children
- Do not ignore fever that lasts more than 2 days
- Do not rely only on herbal mixtures — get the child tested

## Paracetamol Dosage Guide (Syrup)

- Always check the concentration on the bottle (usually 120mg/5ml)
- Under 3 months: See a doctor — do not self-medicate
- 3-12 months: 2.5ml (half a teaspoon)
- 1-5 years: 5-10ml (1-2 teaspoons)
- 6-12 years: 10-20ml (2-4 teaspoons)
- Give every 4-6 hours, maximum 4 doses in 24 hours
- If unsure, ask a pharmacist or health worker

## When It Could Be Malaria

Malaria is the most common cause of fever in Nigerian children. If your child has fever:
1. Take them for a malaria test (RDT or blood test) at the nearest health centre
2. Do not assume it is malaria without testing — other serious conditions have similar symptoms
3. If positive, use ACT (Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy) as prescribed
4. Complete the full course of treatment

## Immunisation Prevents Many Causes of Fever

Make sure your child receives all routine immunisations:
- BCG (tuberculosis) — at birth
- OPV (polio) — at birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks
- Pentavalent (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Hib) — at 6, 10, 14 weeks
- PCV (pneumococcal) — at 6, 10, 14 weeks
- Rotavirus — at 6 and 10 weeks
- Measles — at 9 months and 15 months
- Yellow Fever — at 9 months
- Vitamin A — at 6 months and every 6 months after

Visit your nearest primary health centre for free immunisation.

## Tips

- Keep a thermometer at home
- Know the correct paracetamol dose for your child's age
- Do not wait too long before going to the hospital — febrile convulsions can happen suddenly
- A child who has had a febrile convulsion before is more likely to have another — manage fever early
