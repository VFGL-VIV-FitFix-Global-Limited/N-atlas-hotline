# Childhood Fever and Diarrhoea

Fever and diarrhoea are common in Nigerian children. Most cases can be managed at home, but some signs mean you must go to a hospital immediately.

## Fever in Children

Give paracetamol according to the child's age and weight. Keep the child cool: remove extra clothes, give plenty of fluids, and sponge with lukewarm water. Do not wrap a child tightly when they have fever.

See a doctor or go to hospital if the child has:
- Convulsions or fits
- Very high fever that does not reduce after paracetamol
- Difficulty breathing
- Unusual drowsiness or inability to wake
- A stiff neck or a rash that does not fade when pressed

## Diarrhoea

Give Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) after every loose stool. Continue breastfeeding. Give extra fluids and food. Zinc tablets for 10–14 days help recovery.

Danger signs that need hospital care:
- Blood in the stool
- Repeated vomiting
- Sunken eyes or very dry mouth
- No urine for many hours
- Extreme weakness

## Prevention

Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before feeding a child. Use clean drinking water. Sleep under a treated mosquito net. Complete childhood immunisations at your local clinic.

This is guidance only. A health worker should assess a sick child. For outbreaks, call NCDC on 0800-970-0010. For emergencies, call 112.
