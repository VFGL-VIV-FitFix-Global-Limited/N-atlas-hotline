# HIV/AIDS Awareness and Prevention in Nigeria

## What is HIV?

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a virus that attacks the body's immune system. If not treated, it can lead to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome), where the body can no longer fight infections. HIV has no cure, but with treatment, people living with HIV can live long, healthy lives.

Nigeria has the second-largest HIV burden in the world. However, stigma and misinformation prevent many people from getting tested and treated.

## How HIV Spreads

HIV is transmitted through:
- Unprotected sexual intercourse (vaginal, anal, or oral) with an infected person
- Sharing needles, syringes, or sharp objects contaminated with infected blood
- Mother to child during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding (without treatment)
- Blood transfusion with unscreened blood (rare in properly screened facilities)

## How HIV Does NOT Spread

You CANNOT get HIV from:
- Hugging, handshaking, or touching
- Sharing food, plates, cups, or cutlery
- Mosquito bites
- Coughing or sneezing
- Using the same toilet
- Swimming in the same pool
- Living or working with someone who has HIV

## Prevention

### Use Condoms
- Use a condom correctly every time you have sexual intercourse
- Both male and female condoms are effective
- Free condoms are available at government health centres

### Get Tested with Your Partner
- Know your HIV status and your partner's
- Get tested together before starting a sexual relationship
- Testing is free and confidential at most government health centres

### Prevent Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT)
- All pregnant women should be tested for HIV at their first antenatal visit
- If positive, start antiretroviral (ARV) treatment immediately
- With proper treatment, the chance of passing HIV to the baby is less than 2%
- Follow your doctor's advice on safe infant feeding

### Avoid Sharing Sharp Objects
- Do not share needles, razor blades, clippers, or other sharp objects
- Ensure your barber or hairdresser uses sterilised or new blades
- If you inject drugs, use a new needle every time

### PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)
- PrEP is a daily pill that prevents HIV infection in people who are at high risk
- Available at designated health facilities in Nigeria
- Speak to a health worker if you think you may be at risk

### PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)
- PEP is emergency medication taken within 72 hours after possible exposure to HIV
- Available at hospitals — go immediately if you think you have been exposed
- Must be taken for 28 days
- PEP is NOT a substitute for regular prevention

## HIV Testing

### Where to Get Tested
- Government hospitals and primary health centres (free)
- Private hospitals and clinics
- Community testing events and outreach programmes
- Some pharmacies offer rapid tests

### What to Expect
- A small blood sample is taken (finger prick)
- Rapid test results are ready in 15-20 minutes
- Testing is confidential — your results are private
- Counselling is provided before and after the test

### When to Get Tested
- Before starting a new sexual relationship
- If you have had unprotected sex
- If your partner has HIV or you are unsure of their status
- During pregnancy (at the first antenatal visit)
- If you have symptoms of a weakened immune system (frequent infections, weight loss, persistent cough)
- Regularly, if you are sexually active — at least once a year

## Living with HIV

### Treatment — Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- ART is free in Nigeria at government-designated treatment centres
- ART does not cure HIV, but it controls the virus so you stay healthy
- Take your medication every day at the same time — never skip doses
- With consistent treatment, the virus can become "undetectable," meaning it cannot be passed to others through sex (U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable)

### Staying Healthy with HIV
- Take your ARV medication faithfully every day
- Attend all clinic appointments
- Eat nutritious food
- Exercise regularly
- Avoid alcohol and smoking
- Use condoms to protect your partner
- Get tested and treated for tuberculosis (TB) — TB is the leading cause of death among people living with HIV
- Join a support group — you are not alone

### Stigma
- HIV is a medical condition, not a moral judgment
- People living with HIV deserve dignity, respect, and support
- Discrimination against people with HIV is wrong and in many cases illegal
- If you experience discrimination, contact your state's human rights commission

## HIV and TB

- People living with HIV are more likely to develop tuberculosis (TB)
- TB is curable with proper treatment
- If you have HIV, you should be screened for TB regularly
- If you have TB symptoms (persistent cough for more than 2 weeks, night sweats, weight loss, fever), get tested immediately

## Tips

- Know your status — get tested today
- HIV is not a death sentence — treatment works
- Protect yourself and your partner — use condoms
- Do not buy ARVs from the market — get them free from government treatment centres
- Support, do not stigmatise, people living with HIV
