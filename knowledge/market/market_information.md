# Market Prices and Trading Information

## Understanding Market Prices in Nigeria

Market prices for food commodities in Nigeria vary significantly based on:

### Season
- **Harvest season:** Prices are lowest when supply is abundant (each crop has a different harvest window)
- **Planting/growing season:** Prices increase as stored supplies dwindle
- **Lean season (hunger season):** May to August is when prices for most staples peak

### Location
- Prices are lowest at the point of production (farm gate and nearby markets)
- Prices increase the further you are from the production zone
- Northern markets are cheaper for grains (maize, sorghum, millet, beans, groundnuts)
- Southern and eastern markets are cheaper for roots (cassava, yam) and tree crops (palm oil)

### Quality
- Clean, well-sorted produce commands higher prices
- Foreign matter (stones, chaff, damaged grains) reduces price
- Moisture content matters — dry, well-stored grains sell for more

### Market Type
- **Farm gate:** Cheapest (buying directly from the farmer)
- **Assembly markets:** Where traders buy from farmers in rural areas
- **Wholesale markets:** Where bulk traders buy and sell (e.g., Mile 12, Dawanau)
- **Retail markets:** Where consumers buy — highest prices

## Major Markets in Nigeria

### Grains
- **Dawanau Market, Kano:** Largest grain market in Africa — beans, maize, millet, sorghum, groundnuts
- **Sabo/Gari Market, Kaduna:** Major grain trading hub
- **Custom Market, Maiduguri:** Important for beans and groundnuts

### Root Crops
- **Benue Yam Market, Makurdi:** Major yam trading hub
- **Bodija Market, Ibadan:** Large yam and cassava market
- **Zaki Biam, Benue:** Famous yam market

### Vegetables and Perishables
- **Mile 12, Lagos:** Largest perishable food market in Lagos
- **Kasuwan Barci, Jos:** Major vegetable market (Jos Plateau grows many temperate vegetables)
- **Yankaba, Kano:** Important tomato and pepper market
- **Ogbomoso and Jebba:** Important tomato trading points

### General
- **Onitsha Main Market/Ose Market, Anambra:** One of the largest general markets in West Africa
- **Ogbete Market, Enugu:** Major market in the southeast
- **Wuse Market, Abuja:** Important retail market in the FCT

## Price Information Sources

### Government
- **National Bureau of Statistics (NBS):** Publishes monthly food price data — nigerianstat.gov.ng
- **Federal Ministry of Agriculture:** Monitors commodity prices
- **State Commodity Boards:** Some states publish weekly market prices

### Other Sources
- **FEWS NET (Famine Early Warning Systems Network):** fews.net — monitors food security and prices in Nigeria
- **FAO GIEWS:** Global food price monitoring — fao.org/giews
- **Local radio stations:** Some broadcast market prices weekly

## Tips for Getting Better Prices

### For Farmers (Selling)
- **Do not sell at harvest when everyone is selling** — prices are lowest. If you can store safely, wait 2-3 months.
- **Process before selling** — garri sells for more than raw cassava; milled rice for more than paddy; dried tomatoes for more than fresh
- **Join a cooperative** — sell collectively for better bargaining power
- **Sort and clean your produce** — clean, graded produce sells for 10-20% more
- **Know the market price before you sell** — do not let traders take advantage of you
- **Consider multiple buyers** — do not depend on just one trader

### For Buyers (Purchasing)
- **Buy staples in bulk during harvest season** — you save 30-50%
- **Buy from wholesale markets** — cheaper than retail
- **Buy directly from farmers or cooperatives when possible**
- **Check quality before buying** — moisture content, foreign matter, damage
- **Compare prices between markets and traders**

## Storage to Take Advantage of Price Differences

Proper storage allows you to buy cheap and sell dear, or store your harvest for better prices:

### Grains (Maize, Rice, Beans, Sorghum)
- Dry to 12-13% moisture before storage
- Use hermetic (airtight) storage bags — PICS bags are the most popular in Nigeria
- PICS bags prevent insect damage without chemicals
- Can store for 6-12 months
- Store in a cool, dry, clean room

### Tubers (Yam, Cassava)
- Yam: store in well-ventilated barns, inspect regularly for rot
- Cassava: must be processed within 48 hours — store as garri, flour, or chips
- Potatoes: store in cool, dark, well-ventilated space

### Vegetables and Perishables
- Very difficult to store fresh — sell quickly or process
- Sun-drying (tomatoes, peppers, okra) extends shelf life to months
- Smoking (fish, meat) preserves protein
- Cold storage is ideal but expensive and requires reliable power

## Seasonal Price Calendar (General Pattern)

| Month | Cheap | Expensive |
|---|---|---|
| January-March | Tomatoes, peppers, onions (dry season harvest) | Yam (old stock running out) |
| April-June | — | Most staples rising (planting season) |
| July-August | — | Peak prices for most staples (lean season) |
| August-October | New yam, cassava | Tomatoes, peppers (rainy season scarcity) |
| October-December | Maize, beans, sorghum, groundnuts (harvest) | — |

## Tips

- The biggest profit opportunity in Nigerian agriculture is not growing — it is understanding price patterns and storage
- A farmer who stores maize from October (harvest) to July (peak) can earn 50-100% more
- PICS bags cost about ₦500-₦800 each and pay for themselves many times over
- Listen to market reports on local radio
- Build relationships with traders in major markets
- Consider group marketing through cooperatives — volume gives bargaining power
