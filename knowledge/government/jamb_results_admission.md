# JAMB Results, Cut-Off Marks, and Admission Process

## How JAMB Admission Works

After writing the UTME, admission is a multi-step process:

1. JAMB releases UTME results (usually 2 weeks after the exam)
2. JAMB announces the national minimum cut-off mark
3. Each university, polytechnic, or college sets its own cut-off mark (usually higher than JAMB's minimum)
4. Institutions conduct post-UTME screening (their own test or interview)
5. Institutions select candidates and upload admission lists to JAMB's Central Admissions Processing System (CAPS)
6. JAMB verifies and publishes admission lists
7. Candidates accept or reject offers on the CAPS portal

## How to Check Your JAMB Result

1. Go to jamb.gov.ng
2. Click on "Check UTME Results"
3. Enter your JAMB registration number
4. Your score will appear

You can also check by SMS: send your registration number to 55019.

## What is the Cut-Off Mark?

The national minimum cut-off mark is set by JAMB after a policy meeting with university vice-chancellors, polytechnic rectors, and college provosts. In recent years, it has been around 140 for universities, 100 for polytechnics, and 100 for colleges of education. However, many top universities require 200 or above.

## What is Post-UTME Screening?

Most universities conduct their own screening after JAMB results are released. This may include:
- A written aptitude test
- Verification of O'Level results
- An interview
- Document screening

You must register for your chosen institution's post-UTME screening separately. Check the institution's website for dates and requirements.

## What is CAPS?

The Central Admissions Processing System (CAPS) is JAMB's portal where admission offers are managed. After your chosen institution admits you:

1. Log into your JAMB profile
2. Go to CAPS
3. You will see "ADMITTED" or "NOT ADMITTED"
4. If admitted, click "ACCEPT" to confirm, or "REJECT" if you want to try another institution
5. Print your admission letter from the CAPS portal

## What if You Are Not Admitted?

- You can apply for change of institution or course through the JAMB portal during the supplementary admission period
- You can write JAMB again the following year — there is no limit on attempts
- Consider applying to institutions with lower cut-off marks
