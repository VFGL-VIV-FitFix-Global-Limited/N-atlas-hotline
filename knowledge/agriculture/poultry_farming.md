# Poultry Farming in Nigeria

## Overview

Poultry farming is one of the most popular and profitable livestock businesses in Nigeria. It includes raising chickens for eggs (layers), meat (broilers), or both. Poultry farming can be started with relatively small capital and provides quick returns.

## Types of Poultry Production

### Layers (Egg Production)
- Raised primarily for egg production
- Start laying eggs at 18-22 weeks of age
- Peak production at 25-35 weeks
- Can lay 250-300 eggs per year with good management
- Common breeds: Isa Brown, Lohmann Brown, Bovan Nera, Hy-Line Brown

### Broilers (Meat Production)
- Raised for meat
- Grow fast — ready for market at 6-8 weeks
- Reach 2-3 kg at market weight
- Common breeds: Arbor Acres, Cobb 500, Ross 308, Marshall

### Cockerels
- Male chicks, cheaper to buy than broilers
- Take longer to mature (12-16 weeks)
- Lower feed conversion but still profitable
- Good option for farmers with lower capital

### Indigenous/Local Chickens
- Hardy, disease-resistant, free-range
- Lower production than exotic breeds
- Growing market demand for organic/local chicken (sells at premium)

## Starting a Poultry Farm

### Housing
- Build a poultry house that provides ventilation, shade, and protection from rain and predators
- Face the long side east-west to reduce direct sunlight
- Allow 0.2-0.3 square metres per bird (broilers) or 0.3-0.5 square metres (layers)
- Use wire mesh or bamboo for walls (open-sided housing is best in Nigeria's climate)
- Roof with zinc, aluminium, or thatch
- Ensure good drainage — avoid waterlogged areas
- Deep litter system (wood shavings on the floor) for broilers
- Battery cages or deep litter for layers

### Equipment
- Feeders (one per 25 birds)
- Drinkers/waterers (one per 25 birds)
- Lighting (for layers — 16 hours of light daily increases egg production)
- Egg trays and crates (for layers)
- Heater or brooder (for day-old chicks — charcoal pot or gas brooder)

### Getting Started
1. Prepare your poultry house 2-3 weeks before the birds arrive
2. Clean and disinfect the house
3. Spread wood shavings (litter) on the floor (5-8 cm deep)
4. Set up feeders and drinkers
5. For day-old chicks: set up a brooder with heat source (maintain 33-35°C for the first week, reduce by 2-3°C each week)
6. Buy chicks from a reputable hatchery

## Feeding

### Broilers
| Age | Feed Type | Amount per Bird per Day |
|---|---|---|
| 0-4 weeks | Broiler starter (22-23% protein) | 30-70g, increasing |
| 4-8 weeks | Broiler finisher (19-20% protein) | 80-150g |

Total feed per broiler to market: approximately 4-5 kg

### Layers
| Age | Feed Type |
|---|---|
| 0-8 weeks | Chick mash (20-21% protein) |
| 8-18 weeks | Grower mash (16-17% protein) |
| 18 weeks+ | Layer mash (16-17% protein, high calcium) |

A laying hen eats approximately 110-130g of feed per day.

### Feed Options
- Buy commercial feed from reputable feed mills (Top Feeds, Vital Feeds, Animal Care, CHI, etc.)
- Mix your own feed (cheaper but requires knowledge of formulation):
  - Maize (50-55%), soybean meal (20-25%), groundnut cake, fish meal, bone meal, limestone, salt, vitamins and mineral premix
- Always provide clean, fresh water — birds drink 2-3 times the amount they eat

## Health Management

### Vaccination Schedule (Broilers)
| Day | Vaccine |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Marek's disease (usually done at hatchery) |
| Day 7 | Newcastle disease (NDV — Lasota or Hitchner B1) |
| Day 14 | Gumboro (IBD — Intermediate strain) |
| Day 21 | Newcastle disease booster |
| Day 28 | Gumboro booster |

### Vaccination Schedule (Layers)
| Age | Vaccine |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Marek's disease |
| Week 1 | Newcastle (Hitchner B1) |
| Week 2 | Gumboro |
| Week 3 | Newcastle (Lasota) |
| Week 4 | Gumboro booster |
| Week 8 | Newcastle (Lasota or Komarov) |
| Week 10 | Fowl pox |
| Week 16 | Newcastle (Lasota or killed vaccine) |

### Common Diseases
- **Newcastle Disease:** Most deadly poultry disease in Nigeria. Symptoms: greenish diarrhoea, twisting of neck, sudden death. Prevention: vaccination. No cure — destroy affected birds and disinfect.
- **Gumboro (IBD):** Affects young birds. White watery diarrhoea, weakness. Prevention: vaccination.
- **Coccidiosis:** Bloody diarrhoea, ruffled feathers. Prevention: anticoccidial drugs in feed or water (Amprolium). Keep litter dry.
- **Chronic Respiratory Disease (CRD):** Sneezing, coughing, nasal discharge. Treatment: antibiotics (Tylosin, Erythromycin). Improve ventilation.

### Biosecurity
- Do not allow unauthorised visitors into the poultry house
- Provide a foot dip (disinfectant) at the entrance
- Quarantine new birds for 2 weeks before mixing with existing flock
- Remove dead birds immediately and dispose properly
- Clean and disinfect the house between batches
- Wash hands before and after handling birds

## Economics

### Broilers (500 birds)
- Day-old chicks: ₦250-₦500 each = ₦125,000-₦250,000
- Feed (4-5 kg per bird): approximately ₦350,000-₦500,000
- Vaccines and medication: ₦30,000-₦50,000
- Housing and equipment: ₦200,000-₦500,000 (one-time)
- Total running cost per batch: approximately ₦600,000-₦900,000
- Sales (at ₦3,000-₦5,000 per bird): ₦1,500,000-₦2,500,000
- Profit per batch: ₦500,000-₦1,500,000

### Layers (500 birds)
- Higher initial investment but steady income from eggs
- Each bird produces 250-300 eggs per year
- Revenue from eggs: approximately ₦2,000,000-₦4,000,000 per year
- Plus revenue from spent hens after 18-24 months of laying

## Marketing

- Sell live birds at farm gate, local markets, or to off-takers
- Sell to hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, and event caterers
- Sell eggs to distributors, retailers, and direct consumers
- Festive periods (Christmas, Easter, Sallah) are peak demand — plan production accordingly
- Consider processing (frozen chicken, smoked chicken) for higher margins

## Tips

- Start small and scale up as you gain experience
- Buy chicks from reputable, NAFDAC-certified hatcheries only
- Never skip vaccinations — one outbreak can wipe out your entire flock
- Feed quality directly determines profit — do not use substandard feed
- Good record-keeping is essential — track feed consumption, mortality, egg production, income
- Join a poultry farmers' association for training, bulk purchasing, and market access
- Festive season planning: start broiler production 8-10 weeks before major holidays
