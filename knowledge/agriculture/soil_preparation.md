# Soil Preparation and Management for Nigerian Farmers

## Understanding Your Soil

Healthy soil is the foundation of good farming. Soil provides nutrients, water, and support for plant roots. Nigeria has many different soil types, and understanding your soil helps you make better farming decisions.

## Soil Types in Nigeria

### Sandy Soil
- Light, drains quickly, easy to work
- Found in: parts of the north, coastal areas
- Challenge: does not hold water or nutrients well
- Improvement: add organic matter (manure, compost), mulch heavily

### Clay Soil
- Heavy, holds water, sticky when wet, hard when dry
- Found in: parts of the middle belt, river valleys
- Challenge: waterlogging, hard to plough when dry
- Improvement: add organic matter, sand, and gypsum to improve drainage

### Loamy Soil
- Best for farming — balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay
- Holds water and nutrients well, drains properly
- Found in: various parts of Nigeria
- This is what you want to create — add organic matter to move any soil closer to loam

### Laterite (Red Soil)
- Common in forest and guinea savanna zones
- Often low in nutrients, can be acidite
- Improvement: add lime (to reduce acidity), organic matter, and appropriate fertiliser

## Land Clearing

### Manual Clearing
- Cut down bush and trees with cutlass (machete)
- Remove stumps if possible
- Gather and burn debris (or better — compost it)
- Most common method for smallholder farmers

### Mechanised Clearing
- Use tractor with plough and harrow
- Faster and more thorough
- Tractor hire is available through state ADP or private operators
- Cost: ₦15,000-₦40,000 per hectare (varies by location)

### After Clearing
- Do not burn all residues — leave some to decompose and add organic matter
- If you must burn, do it before the rains to allow nutrients to wash into the soil

## Tillage (Working the Soil)

### Why Till
- Loosens compacted soil for root growth
- Mixes in organic matter and fertiliser
- Prepares a good seedbed
- Controls weeds

### Methods
- **Manual:** Hoe, cutlass, shovel — labour-intensive but common for small farms
- **Animal-drawn:** Ox plough — still used in some northern areas
- **Tractor:** Ploughing and harrowing — fastest and most thorough

### Making Ridges
- Ridges are raised rows of soil, commonly used in Nigeria
- Good for: yam, cassava, maize, beans, vegetables
- Helps drainage, makes weeding easier, concentrates soil for root crops
- Make ridges 20-30 cm high, 60-100 cm apart (depending on crop)
- Ridges should run across the slope (not downhill) to reduce erosion

### Making Mounds
- Traditional method for yam farming
- Mounds are 60-90 cm high, spaced 1m apart
- Labour-intensive but effective for root crops

### Minimum/Zero Tillage
- Planting without ploughing, using herbicides to control weeds
- Preserves soil structure and reduces erosion
- Requires herbicides (Paraquat or Glyphosate for land clearing, then plant directly)
- Growing in popularity for maize and cassava

## Soil Fertility Management

### Organic Matter
Organic matter is the most important thing you can add to any soil. It:
- Improves water-holding capacity
- Provides nutrients slowly over time
- Improves soil structure
- Supports beneficial soil organisms

**Sources:**
- Composted animal manure (poultry, cow, goat)
- Crop residues (maize stalks, rice straw, bean haulms)
- Compost from kitchen waste and plant material
- Green manure (legume cover crops ploughed in)

### How to Make Compost
1. Choose a shady spot near your farm
2. Dig a pit (1m wide, 1m long, 0.5m deep) or build a heap on the ground
3. Layer: plant material (grass, leaves, crop waste) + animal manure + thin layer of topsoil
4. Wet each layer (moist but not soaking)
5. Continue layering until the pit is full or the heap is 1-1.5m high
6. Cover with a thin layer of soil or old sacking
7. Turn the heap every 2-3 weeks
8. Compost is ready in 2-3 months (dark, crumbly, earthy smell)

### Crop Rotation
- Alternate between crops that take nutrients and crops that add them
- Always include a legume (beans, groundnuts, soybeans, cowpea) in your rotation — legumes fix nitrogen from the air into the soil
- Do not grow the same crop in the same field every year

### Cover Cropping
- Plant cover crops (mucuna, centrosema, cowpea) during the off-season or in fallow fields
- Cover crops prevent erosion, suppress weeds, and add organic matter and nitrogen when ploughed in
- Mucuna is especially effective — it can dramatically improve poor soil in just one season

## Soil Erosion Control

Erosion is a major problem in Nigeria, especially on slopes and in high-rainfall areas.

### How to Prevent Erosion
- Make ridges across the slope (contour ridging), not downhill
- Plant cover crops on bare land
- Mulch around crops
- Plant trees and hedgerows along the edges of fields
- Build simple drainage channels to direct water away from fields
- Leave crop residues on the field after harvest
- Do not burn crop residues

### Terracing
- On steep slopes, build terraces (stepped levels) to slow water flow
- Can be done with earth bunds (small ridges along the contour)
- Vetiver grass planted along contour lines is very effective at stabilising slopes

## Soil Testing

A soil test tells you exactly what your soil has and what it needs.

### How to Collect a Soil Sample
1. Collect soil from 10-15 spots across your field
2. Use a hoe or trowel to dig 15-20 cm deep
3. Take a handful from each spot
4. Mix all samples together in a clean bucket
5. Take about 0.5-1 kg of the mixed sample
6. Put in a clean bag labelled with your name, field location, and date
7. Send to a soil testing laboratory

### Where to Test
- State ADP laboratories
- University agricultural laboratories
- IITA, Ibadan
- National Root Crops Research Institute (NRCRI), Umudike
- Private agricultural laboratories
- Cost: approximately ₦5,000-₦15,000

## Tips

- Healthy soil grows healthy crops — invest in your soil
- Add organic matter every season — it is the single most important thing you can do
- Do not burn crop residues — return them to the soil
- Start a compost heap — it costs nothing and improves your soil enormously
- Test your soil at least once — it tells you exactly what to add and saves money on fertiliser
- Erosion takes away your best topsoil — prevent it with contour farming and cover crops
- Prepare your land early — before the rains, so you can plant on time
