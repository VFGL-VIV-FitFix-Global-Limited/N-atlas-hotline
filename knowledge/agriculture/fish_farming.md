# Fish Farming (Aquaculture) in Nigeria

## Overview

Fish farming is one of the fastest-growing agricultural businesses in Nigeria. Nigeria is the largest consumer of fish in Africa but imports a huge amount to meet demand, creating a massive opportunity for local fish farmers. Catfish and tilapia are the most commonly farmed species.

## Species

### Catfish (Clarias gariepinus and Heterobranchus)
- Most popular farmed fish in Nigeria
- Hardy, fast-growing, tolerates poor water quality
- Market size: 0.5-2 kg (reached in 4-8 months)
- Clarias grows faster, Heterobranchus grows larger
- Heteroclarias (hybrid of both) combines fast growth and large size

### Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
- Second most popular
- Grows well in ponds and cages
- Mild flavour, good market demand
- Males grow faster than females — monosex culture (all-male) is recommended
- Market size: 200-500g (reached in 6-8 months)

## Production Systems

### Earthen Ponds
- Dug in the ground, lined with clay or plastic
- Best for large-scale production
- Size: from 100 to 1000+ square metres
- Water must be available (borehole, river, or rainwater)
- Advantages: cheaper to construct, more natural, lower stress on fish
- Disadvantages: requires more land, harder to manage water quality

### Concrete/Block Ponds (Tanks)
- Built with blocks and cement, plastered inside
- Popular in urban and peri-urban areas
- Size: typically 2m x 2m to 5m x 10m, 1-1.5m deep
- Can stock 20-50 fish per square metre with good water management
- Advantages: easier to manage, can be built in small spaces
- Disadvantages: higher construction cost, requires regular water change

### Tarpaulin Ponds
- Mobile, collapsible ponds made of reinforced tarpaulin
- Very popular for beginners due to low cost
- Size: various, commonly 2m x 2m to 4m x 6m
- Stock at 20-40 fish per square metre
- Advantages: cheap, easy to set up, movable
- Disadvantages: shorter lifespan (2-3 years), can tear, water heats up quickly

### Cage Culture
- Fish raised in floating cages in rivers, lakes, or reservoirs
- Growing in popularity, especially in dam areas
- Requires permission from the local authority

## Getting Started

### Site Selection
- Reliable water source (borehole is best for consistent quality)
- Good drainage
- Access to road for transporting feed and fish
- Proximity to market
- Security

### Stocking
- Buy fingerlings (juvenile fish, 3-6 cm) or juveniles (6-10 cm) from reputable hatcheries
- Stock size: 500-2000 fish for beginners
- Stocking density:
  - Earthen ponds: 5-10 fish per square metre
  - Concrete tanks: 20-50 fish per square metre (with good water management)
  - Tarpaulin: 20-40 fish per square metre
- Sort fish by size every 4-6 weeks to prevent cannibalism (catfish are cannibals)

## Feeding

### Feed Types
- **Commercial floating feed** (recommended): Coppens, Durante, Vital, Top Feeds
- **Local feed:** Can be formulated from soybean meal, groundnut cake, fish meal, maize, vitamin premix — requires knowledge of formulation

### Feeding Schedule
| Stage | Fish Size | Feed Size | Feeding Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fingerling | 3-10g | 0.5-1mm | 5-10% body weight, 3-4 times daily |
| Juvenile | 10-100g | 2mm | 3-5% body weight, 3 times daily |
| Grower | 100-500g | 4mm | 2-3% body weight, 2-3 times daily |
| Finisher | 500g+ | 6-9mm | 1.5-2% body weight, 2 times daily |

### Feed Tips
- Feed is the biggest cost (60-70% of total cost) — manage it carefully
- Do not overfeed — uneaten food pollutes the water
- Feed at the same time every day
- Reduce feeding in cold weather and when water quality is poor
- A well-fed catfish reaches 1 kg in 5-6 months

## Water Management

### Water Quality Parameters
- **Temperature:** 25-30°C (optimal for catfish and tilapia)
- **pH:** 6.5-8.5
- **Dissolved oxygen:** Above 3 mg/L (low oxygen kills fish)
- **Ammonia:** Below 0.05 mg/L (fish waste produces ammonia — toxic at high levels)

### Maintaining Good Water
- Change 20-30% of water weekly (concrete/tarpaulin ponds)
- Use aerators if possible (especially at high stocking density)
- Do not overfeed — excess feed decomposes and reduces water quality
- Remove dead fish immediately
- Clean pond bottom periodically
- In earthen ponds: maintain water level, manage aquatic weeds

### Signs of Poor Water Quality
- Fish gasping at the surface (low oxygen)
- Fish not eating
- Cloudy, foul-smelling water
- Fish mortality

## Health Management

### Common Diseases
- **Bacterial infections:** Symptoms include sores, red spots, fin rot. Treatment: antibiotics in water (oxytetracycline) or salt bath
- **Fungal infections:** White cotton-like growth on body. Treatment: salt bath (10g/L for 15-30 minutes) or potassium permanganate
- **Parasites:** Scratching against surfaces, white spots. Treatment: salt bath, formalin treatment

### Prevention
- Buy fingerlings from disease-free hatcheries
- Maintain good water quality
- Do not overstock
- Quarantine new fish before adding to existing stock
- Handle fish gently during sorting and transfer
- Remove dead fish immediately

## Harvesting and Marketing

### Harvesting
- Catfish: harvest at 0.5-2 kg (4-8 months, depending on feeding and management)
- Tilapia: harvest at 200-500g (6-8 months)
- Drain the pond or use a net to harvest
- Harvest early in the morning when it is cool
- Keep harvested fish alive in a holding tank if selling live

### Marketing
- Live fish sells at the highest price
- Sell at farm gate, local markets, or to off-takers
- Supply restaurants, hotels, and suya joints
- Sell smoked fish (dried) — longer shelf life, good market
- Festive periods (Christmas, Easter, weddings) are peak demand
- Average price: ₦1,500-₦3,000 per kg (live catfish)

## Economics (1000 Catfish)

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Pond setup (tarpaulin/concrete) | ₦100,000-₦500,000 (one-time) |
| Fingerlings (1000 pieces) | ₦30,000-₦80,000 |
| Feed (to market size) | ₦300,000-₦500,000 |
| Water and power | ₦30,000-₦60,000 |
| Medication and maintenance | ₦20,000-₦40,000 |
| **Total running cost** | **₦380,000-₦680,000** |

| **Estimated profit** | **₦900,000-₦1,200,000** |

(Actual results vary based on management, feed costs, and selling price.)

## Tips

- Start with catfish — it is hardy and forgiving for beginners
- Feed quality is everything — do not use poor-quality feed to save money
- Water management is the second most critical factor after feeding
- Sort fish regularly to prevent cannibalism (especially catfish)
- Start small (500-1000 fish) and learn before scaling up
- Smoking fish adds value and extends shelf life
- Keep detailed records of feed purchases, fish growth, water changes, and sales
