# Agricultural Loans and Finance in Nigeria

## Overview

Access to finance is one of the biggest challenges for Nigerian farmers. Several government programmes, development banks, and microfinance institutions provide loans and grants to help farmers start or expand their operations.

## Government Loan Programmes

### Anchor Borrowers' Programme (ABP)
- **Who:** Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)
- **For:** Smallholder farmers producing specific crops (rice, wheat, maize, cotton, cassava, poultry, fish, etc.)
- **How it works:** Farmers receive inputs (seeds, fertiliser, chemicals) on credit through their cooperatives or farmer groups, tied to an anchor (large buyer/processor) who guarantees off-take
- **Interest rate:** Single digit (currently around 9%)
- **How to apply:** Through a registered farmers' cooperative linked to an anchor. Contact your state ADP or the nearest CBN branch

### Nigeria Incentive-Based Risk Sharing System for Agricultural Lending (NIRSAL)
- **Who:** NIRSAL PLC (owned by CBN)
- **For:** Farmers, agribusinesses, and agricultural enterprises at all levels
- **What it offers:**
  - Credit Risk Guarantee: guarantees part of the loan so banks are willing to lend to farmers
  - Interest drawback: refunds part of the interest if you repay on time
  - Technical assistance: training and support
- **How to apply:** Apply through any commercial bank. NIRSAL provides the guarantee, the bank provides the loan.
- **Website:** nirsal.com

### Bank of Agriculture (BOA)
- **Who:** Federal Government-owned development bank
- **For:** Individual farmers, cooperatives, and agribusinesses
- **Loan types:**
  - On-lending (through cooperatives and microfinance banks)
  - Direct lending to farmers and agribusinesses
- **Interest rate:** Single digit (typically 5-9%)
- **Collateral:** May require land, equipment, or group guarantee
- **How to apply:** Visit the nearest BOA branch with your farm plan, identification, and cooperative membership details
- **Website:** bfrn.org.ng

### Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Fund (ACGSF)
- **Who:** CBN through commercial and microfinance banks
- **For:** Individual farmers and cooperatives
- **What it offers:** Government guarantees 75% of the loan, making banks willing to lend
- **Maximum loan:**
  - Individual: up to ₦50 million
  - Cooperative: up to ₦1 billion
- **Interest rate:** Below commercial rates
- **How to apply:** Apply at any commercial bank or microfinance bank participating in the scheme

### AGSMEIS (Agri-Business/Small and Medium Enterprise Investment Scheme)
- **Who:** CBN through participating financial institutions
- **For:** Agricultural startups and small businesses
- **Maximum loan:** Up to ₦10 million
- **Interest rate:** 5%
- **Requirement:** Complete a SMEDAN or EDC training programme before applying
- **How to apply:** Through participating banks after training certification

## State Government Programmes

Many states have their own agricultural loan and support programmes:
- Contact your state Ministry of Agriculture
- Contact your state Agricultural Development Programme (ADP)
- Check your state government website for current programmes
- Some states offer free or subsidised inputs (seedlings, fertiliser, chemicals) instead of cash loans

## Microfinance and Cooperative Options

### Microfinance Banks
- Many microfinance banks offer agricultural loans
- Typically smaller amounts (₦100,000-₦5,000,000)
- Higher interest rates than government programmes (15-30%)
- Less paperwork and faster approval
- LAPO, Accion Microfinance, and FarmFundr are examples

### Cooperatives
- Join or form a farmers' cooperative
- Many loan programmes only lend to cooperatives, not individuals
- Cooperatives provide:
  - Group guarantee for loans (members guarantee each other)
  - Bulk purchasing of inputs at lower prices
  - Collective marketing for better prices
  - Access to government programmes
- Register your cooperative with your state Ministry of Commerce

### Crowdfunding and Agritech Platforms
- Growing number of platforms connect investors with farmers
- Platforms like Farmcrowdy, ThriveAgric, and FarmFundr
- Farmers get funding; investors get returns
- Read terms carefully before participating

## How to Prepare a Loan Application

### What You Need
1. **Farm plan or business plan** — what you will farm, on what land, expected costs, expected revenue
2. **Identification** — NIN, voter's card, driver's licence, or international passport
3. **Proof of farming activity** — photos of your farm, lease agreement for land, cooperative membership
4. **Bank account** — open a bank or microfinance bank account in your farm or cooperative name
5. **Cooperative registration** — for group loans
6. **Training certificate** — some programmes require SMEDAN, IITA, or ADP training completion
7. **Collateral** — may include land title, certificate of occupancy, equipment, or group guarantee

### Tips for a Strong Application
- Be realistic about your costs and expected revenue
- Show that you have experience or training in farming
- Have a clear repayment plan
- Keep records of your farming activities
- A cooperative application is often stronger than an individual one

## Repayment

- Always repay your loan on time — it builds your credit history and access to future, larger loans
- Late payment attracts penalties and can prevent you from getting future loans
- Some programmes offer grace periods (no payment due during the growing season)
- If you are having difficulty repaying, contact the lender early — restructuring is easier than defaulting

## Insurance

### Nigerian Agricultural Insurance Corporation (NAIC)
- Provides crop and livestock insurance to protect against losses from natural disasters, pests, and diseases
- Premium: typically 2-5% of the insured value
- Government may subsidise part of the premium
- Required by some loan programmes
- Contact: naic.gov.ng

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Do not divert loan funds to non-farming activities — this is the most common reason farmers fail to repay
- Do not borrow more than your farm can service
- Do not skip the farm plan — lenders need to see that your business makes sense
- Do not avoid cooperatives — most government programmes require cooperative membership
- Do not be afraid to ask questions — understand the terms before signing

## Tips

- Join a cooperative — this is the single most important step for accessing agricultural finance
- Start small, prove your ability to repay, then scale up
- Keep records — income, expenses, yields — lenders and support programmes want to see them
- Complete training programmes — many loan programmes require them, and they improve your farming too
- Register with your state ADP — this puts you in the system for government support
- NIRSAL does not lend directly — it helps you get a loan from a bank by guaranteeing part of the risk
