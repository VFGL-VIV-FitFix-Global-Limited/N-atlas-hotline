# Planting Seasons by Crop and Region

Nigeria has two broad rainfall patterns. The south has a longer rainy season. The north has a shorter, sharper season. Plant with reliable rains, not the first isolated shower.

## Southern Nigeria (approx. March–October rains)

- Maize: March–April (early) and August (late season in some areas)
- Cassava: March–May
- Rice (upland): March–May
- Yam: November–March (setts in dry season / early rains depending on zone)
- Vegetables: start of rains; dry-season vegetable farming needs irrigation

## Northern Nigeria (approx. May–September rains)

- Maize: May–June
- Millet and sorghum: May–June
- Rice (fadama/lowland): with available water, often June–July transplanting
- Cowpea: June–July
- Groundnut: May–June

## Practical Advice

Ask your local ADP extension officer — they know this year's rainfall outlook. Climate is shifting; planting dates can move by weeks. Use improved seed suited to your zone. Avoid planting on flood-prone land without drainage.
