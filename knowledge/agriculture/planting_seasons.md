# Planting Seasons and Crop Calendar for Nigeria

## Nigeria's Climate Zones

Nigeria has diverse climate zones affecting planting times:

### Southern Nigeria (Forest/Coastal Zone)
- Two rainy seasons: March-July (major) and September-November (minor)
- Annual rainfall: 1,500-4,000mm
- States: Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Osun, Ondo, Ekiti, Edo, Delta, Bayelsa, Rivers, Cross River, Akwa Ibom, Imo, Abia, Anambra, Enugu, Ebonyi

### Middle Belt (Guinea Savanna)
- One rainy season: April-October
- Annual rainfall: 1,000-1,500mm
- States: Kwara, Kogi, Benue, Niger, Nasarawa, Plateau, Taraba, Adamawa, FCT

### Northern Nigeria (Sudan/Sahel Savanna)
- Short rainy season: June-September
- Annual rainfall: 500-1,000mm
- States: Kaduna, Kano, Katsina, Zamfara, Sokoto, Kebbi, Jigawa, Bauchi, Gombe, Yobe, Borno

## Key Principles

### Timing Matters
- Plant at the onset of the rains — the first 2-3 good rains signal planting time
- Early planting takes advantage of early rains and avoids late-season pest pressure
- Late planting reduces yields and increases pest and disease risk
- In the north, the rainy season is short — timing is critical

### Soil Preparation
- Prepare land before the rains arrive
- Clear, plough, and make ridges or mounds during the dry season
- Apply organic manure during land preparation
- Early preparation means you can plant as soon as the rains start

### Crop Rotation
Rotating crops improves soil fertility and breaks pest and disease cycles.

Good rotations:
- Year 1: Maize → Year 2: Beans/Groundnut → Year 3: Cassava or vegetables
- Year 1: Rice → Year 2: Soybeans → Year 3: Maize
- Year 1: Yam → Year 2: Maize + beans → Year 3: Cassava
- Year 1: Tomato → Year 2: Maize → Year 3: Pepper/okra (avoid back-to-back solanaceous crops)

Always include a legume (beans, groundnuts, soybeans, cowpea) in your rotation — they fix nitrogen in the soil.

### Intercropping

Common intercropping combinations in Nigeria:
- Maize + beans or cowpea (beans fix nitrogen for the maize)
- Cassava + maize + melon (three tiers of crops using different light levels)
- Yam + maize + vegetables
- Plantain + cocoyam + vegetables (in the south)

Benefits:
- Maximises use of farmland
- Reduces risk — if one crop fails, the other may still produce
- Can improve pest control
- Improves soil cover and reduces erosion

## Dry Season Farming (Irrigated)

Dry season farming is increasingly important and often more profitable than wet season farming:

### Water Sources
- Rivers and streams
- Irrigation dams and canals (fadama areas)
- Boreholes with pumps
- Wells

### Best Dry Season Crops
- Tomatoes (best quality, highest prices)
- Peppers
- Onions (especially in the north)
- Vegetables (spinach, lettuce, cabbage, carrots)
- Rice (irrigated paddies)
- Wheat (northern Nigeria)
- Watermelon

### Fadama Farming
- Fadama areas are low-lying flood plains near rivers — naturally irrigated
- Very productive for dry season farming
- National Fadama Development Programme provides support to fadama farmers
- Contact your state ADP for Fadama programme information

## Tips

- Listen to weather forecasts from the Nigerian Meteorological Agency (NiMet) for seasonal predictions
- Do not wait for the "perfect" rains — plant after 2-3 consistent rains
- Keep records of planting dates and yields each year — this helps you make better decisions
- Dry season farming can be more profitable because supply is low and prices are high
- Climate change is shifting rainfall patterns — be prepared to adjust your planting calendar
- Your local extension worker knows the best planting times for your specific area — consult them

## Where to Get Help
