# Cassava Farming in Nigeria

## Overview

Cassava is the most widely grown crop in Nigeria and a major source of food and income. Nigeria is the world's largest producer of cassava. It grows well in most parts of Nigeria, tolerates poor soil, and is relatively drought-resistant.

## Varieties

### Improved Varieties (Recommended)
- **TME 419** — high yielding, early maturity (10-12 months), good for garri and fufu
- **TMS 30572** — popular, good yield, good for garri
- **TMS 98/0505** — high starch content, good for industrial use
- **NR 8082** — disease-resistant, good yield
- **IITA improved varieties** — check with your state Agricultural Development Programme (ADP) for the latest releases

Improved varieties yield 20-40 tonnes per hectare, compared to 8-12 tonnes for local varieties.

## Planting

### When to Plant
- Plant at the beginning of the rainy season: March to May (southern Nigeria) or May to July (northern Nigeria)
- Can also be planted in September-October in areas with two rainy seasons

### Land Preparation
1. Clear the land of bush, stumps, and debris
2. Plough and harrow if possible, or make mounds/ridges
3. Ridges or mounds help drainage and make harvesting easier
4. If you can afford it, do a soil test through your state ADP

### Planting Material
- Use healthy stem cuttings (called "setts" or "stakes") from mature, disease-free plants
- Cut stems into 20-25 cm lengths, each with at least 5 nodes
- Plant cuttings at a 45-degree angle, burying about two-thirds in the soil
- Spacing: 1m x 1m (10,000 plants per hectare) or 1m x 0.8m for higher density

### Planting Steps
1. Make mounds or ridges about 30cm high
2. Insert stem cuttings at a 45-degree angle
3. Ensure the buds point upward
4. Press soil firmly around the cutting
5. One cutting per mound or ridge position

## Crop Management

### Weeding
- Weed 2-3 times during the growing season
- First weeding at 3-4 weeks after planting
- Second weeding at 8-10 weeks
- Third weeding at 12-14 weeks (if needed)
- Weeding is critical in the first 3 months — cassava cannot compete well with weeds early on
- Herbicides (pre-emergence): Primextra or Atrazine can be applied just after planting

### Fertiliser
- Although cassava grows on poor soil, fertiliser significantly increases yield
- Apply NPK 15:15:15 at 400kg per hectare
- Apply 6-8 weeks after planting, in a ring around the plant (not directly on the stem)
- Organic manure (poultry droppings, compost) is also effective and cheaper

### Intercropping
- Cassava can be intercropped with maize, melon (egusi), vegetables, or yam
- Intercropping provides additional income and food
- Space cassava wider (1.5m x 1m) when intercropping

## Pests and Diseases

### Major Diseases
- **Cassava Mosaic Disease (CMD):** Causes leaf curling and yellowing, stunted growth. Use resistant varieties (TME 419, TMS 98/0505). Remove and burn infected plants.
- **Cassava Bacterial Blight (CBB):** Causes wilting and stem dieback. Use clean planting material and resistant varieties.
- **Cassava Brown Streak Disease (CBSD):** Causes root rot. Use resistant varieties and certified clean planting material.

### Major Pests
- **Cassava Mealybug:** White cotton-like insects on stems and leaves. Biological control (parasitic wasps released by IITA) is effective.
- **Cassava Green Mite:** Causes leaf damage. Resistant varieties and predatory mites help control.
- **Grasshoppers and termites:** Apply recommended insecticides if severe.

### Prevention
- Plant disease-resistant improved varieties
- Use clean, healthy planting material
- Practice crop rotation — do not plant cassava on the same land every year
- Remove and destroy diseased plants

## Harvesting

- Cassava is ready for harvest 9-18 months after planting, depending on variety
- Improved varieties can be harvested from 10-12 months
- Harvest by pulling up the plant and removing tubers from the stem
- Harvest in dry weather for easier pulling
- Process or sell within 48 hours — cassava tubers deteriorate quickly after harvest

## Processing

Cassava can be processed into many products:
- **Garri:** Peeled, grated, fermented, pressed, sieved, and fried
- **Fufu:** Peeled, soaked, fermented, and sieved
- **Cassava flour:** Peeled, grated, pressed, dried, and milled (for baking and cooking)
- **Starch:** Extracted from grated cassava
- **Cassava chips:** Peeled, sliced, and dried (for animal feed or industrial use)
- **Ethanol:** Industrial use

Processing adds significant value — processed cassava products sell for 2-5 times the price of raw tubers.

## Economics

- **Cost of production:** approximately ₦150,000-₦300,000 per hectare
- **Expected yield:** 20-40 tonnes per hectare with improved varieties
- **Selling price:** ₦30,000-₦60,000 per tonne (raw tubers, varies by season and location)
- **Revenue potential:** ₦600,000-₦2,000,000+ per hectare
- Processing into garri or flour increases value significantly

## Where to Get Help

- State Agricultural Development Programme (ADP)
- International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA): iita.org
- Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development
- Local Government Agriculture Department
- Agricultural extension workers in your area

## Tips

- Always use improved varieties — the yield difference is enormous
- Get your planting material from a reliable source (IITA, state ADP, or certified seed producers)
- Do not plant cuttings from diseased plants
- Weed early and often in the first 3 months
- Process your cassava to earn more — raw tubers lose value within days
- Join a cooperative for better access to inputs, credit, and markets
