# Cassava Farming Guide

Cassava is a staple crop across Nigeria. It grows on many soil types and can survive dry spells better than maize.

## Planting Season

In the south, plant at the start of the rains (March to May). In the north, plant with the rains (May to July). Cassava needs well-drained soil. Avoid waterlogged fields.

## Variety Selection

Recommended improved varieties include TMS 30572 and TME 419. They yield more and resist some diseases better than local types. Get cuttings from your state Agricultural Development Programme (ADP) or a trusted farmer.

## How to Plant

- Cut stems into 20–25 cm pieces with 5–7 nodes
- Plant at an angle or upright, 1 metre between plants and 1 metre between rows
- Weed 3–4 times in the first 3–4 months

## Common Pests and Diseases

Watch for cassava mosaic disease (yellow mottled leaves) and mealybugs. Use clean cuttings from healthy plants. Report severe outbreaks to your ADP office.

## Harvest

Most improved varieties are ready from 9 to 12 months. Harvest when roots are mature but before they become woody. Process quickly into gari, fufu or chips to avoid spoilage.

For local advice, contact your ADP office or the Federal Ministry of Agriculture.
