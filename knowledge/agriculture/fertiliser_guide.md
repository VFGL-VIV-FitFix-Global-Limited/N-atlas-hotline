# Fertiliser Guide for Nigerian Farmers

## What is Fertiliser?

Fertiliser is any material added to soil to supply nutrients that plants need to grow. Using the right fertiliser at the right time and in the right amount can double or triple your crop yield.

## Types of Fertiliser

### Inorganic (Chemical) Fertilisers

**NPK Fertiliser**
- Contains Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K)
- Common formulations in Nigeria: NPK 15:15:15, NPK 20:10:10, NPK 12:12:17
- The numbers show the percentage of each nutrient (e.g., NPK 15:15:15 has 15% nitrogen, 15% phosphorus, 15% potassium)
- Best for: all crops at planting or early growth

**Urea (46% Nitrogen)**
- Highest concentration of nitrogen
- Used for top-dressing (applied after planting to boost growth)
- Best for: cereals (maize, rice, wheat, millet), vegetables
- Apply when soil is moist — urea can be lost to the air if applied on dry soil

**CAN (Calcium Ammonium Nitrate)**
- Contains 27% nitrogen plus calcium
- Less likely to be lost to the air than urea
- Good alternative to urea for top-dressing

**Single Super Phosphate (SSP)**
- Contains 18% phosphorus
- Applied at planting to promote root growth
- Good for: legumes (beans, groundnuts, soybeans)

**Muriate of Potash (MOP)**
- Contains 60% potassium
- Applied to improve fruit quality, disease resistance, and tuber formation
- Good for: yam, cassava, potatoes, tomatoes

### Organic Fertilisers

**Poultry Manure**
- Richest animal manure — high in nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium
- Apply well-decomposed (composted for at least 4-6 weeks)
- Application rate: 5-10 tonnes per hectare
- Cheap and available from poultry farms

**Cow Dung**
- Lower nutrient content than poultry manure but excellent for improving soil structure
- Must be well-decomposed before use
- Application rate: 10-20 tonnes per hectare

**Compost**
- Made from decomposed plant materials, food waste, and animal manure
- Improves soil structure, water-holding capacity, and nutrient content
- Takes 2-3 months to prepare
- Every farmer should have a compost heap

**Green Manure**
- Grow leguminous cover crops (mucuna, centrosema, cowpea) and plough them into the soil before planting your main crop
- Adds nitrogen and organic matter to the soil

## How to Apply Fertiliser

### General Rules
1. Apply when the soil is moist — not dry, not waterlogged
2. Do not place fertiliser directly on the plant or seed — it will burn
3. Place fertiliser 5-8 cm away from the plant base
4. Cover with soil after application (especially urea)
5. Split application is better than one-time — apply in 2-3 doses

### Methods
- **Ring application:** Make a shallow trench in a ring around the plant, place fertiliser, cover with soil
- **Side banding:** Place fertiliser in a shallow furrow along the row, 5-8 cm from plants
- **Broadcasting:** Spread evenly across the field (for basal application before planting)
- **Foliar application:** Spray liquid fertiliser on leaves (for micronutrients)

## Fertiliser Recommendations by Crop

| Crop | At Planting | Top Dressing | When to Top Dress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maize | NPK 15:15:15, 100 kg/ha | Urea, 100 kg/ha | 4-6 weeks |
| Rice | NPK 15:15:15, 200 kg/ha | Urea, 50 kg/ha (twice) | 3-4 weeks and 8 weeks |
| Cassava | NPK 15:15:15, 400 kg/ha | - | - |
| Yam | NPK 15:15:15, 300 kg/ha | - | - |
| Tomato | NPK 15:15:15, 300 kg/ha | Urea/CAN, 150 kg/ha | 3 weeks and at flowering |
| Pepper | NPK 15:15:15, 200 kg/ha | Urea, 100 kg/ha | 4-6 weeks |
| Beans/Cowpea | SSP, 100 kg/ha | - | - |
| Groundnut | SSP, 200 kg/ha | - | - |

## Government Fertiliser Programmes

### E-Wallet / Growth Enhancement Support Scheme (GES)
- Government-subsidised fertiliser distributed through registered farmers
- Register with your state ADP to access subsidised inputs
- Prices are typically 50-70% of market price

### How to Access Subsidised Fertiliser
1. Register as a farmer with your state ADP or Federal Ministry of Agriculture
2. Get a Farmer ID card
3. When fertiliser is available, you will be notified
4. Purchase at the subsidised price from designated distribution points
5. Some programmes require a mobile phone for verification

## Common Mistakes

1. **Applying too much:** Over-fertilisation wastes money and can burn plants or pollute water
2. **Applying at the wrong time:** Late application is wasted — apply when the crop needs it most
3. **Placing directly on the plant:** Burns roots and stems — always apply away from the plant base
4. **Using only one type:** Crops need multiple nutrients — NPK is balanced, but different stages have different needs
5. **Ignoring organic sources:** Organic fertiliser improves soil health long-term — chemical fertiliser alone degrades soil over time
6. **Not checking soil:** A soil test tells you exactly what your soil needs, avoiding waste

## Soil Testing

- Contact your state ADP or a soil testing laboratory
- Collect soil samples from different spots in your field (10-15 cm deep)
- Mix the samples together and send to the lab
- The lab report will tell you what nutrients your soil lacks and recommend the right fertiliser
- Soil testing costs approximately ₦5,000-₦15,000 per sample

## Storage Tips

- Store fertiliser in a cool, dry place, off the ground (on pallets or wooden boards)
- Keep away from direct sunlight and rain
- Do not store near food, animal feed, or fuel
- Keep away from children
- Use within the season — old fertiliser may lose effectiveness

## Tips

- Combine organic and inorganic fertiliser for best results
- Start with a soil test if possible — it saves money in the long run
- Timing and placement are as important as the amount
- Build a compost heap — it is the cheapest way to improve your soil
- Register with your state ADP to access subsidised fertiliser
- Join a farmers' cooperative for bulk purchases at lower prices
