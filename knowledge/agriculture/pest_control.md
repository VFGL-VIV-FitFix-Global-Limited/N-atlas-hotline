# Pest and Disease Control for Nigerian Farmers

## Understanding Pests and Diseases

Pests (insects, rodents, birds, weeds) and diseases (fungal, bacterial, viral) cause enormous crop losses in Nigeria — estimated at 30-50% of potential harvest. Early detection and proper management are critical.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM combines multiple methods to control pests effectively while minimising chemical use. The steps, in order of preference:

### 1. Prevention (First Line of Defence)
- Use disease-resistant varieties
- Plant certified, disease-free seeds and planting material
- Practice crop rotation — do not grow the same crop in the same field every season
- Keep the field clean — remove crop residues and weeds
- Plant on time — early planting often avoids peak pest periods
- Space plants correctly — overcrowding promotes disease

### 2. Cultural Control
- Intercropping: growing two crops together can reduce pest damage (e.g., maize + beans)
- Trap cropping: planting a preferred host crop around the main field to attract pests away
- Handpicking: removing insects, egg masses, or diseased plants by hand
- Proper weeding: many weeds harbour pests and diseases
- Water management: avoiding waterlogging reduces fungal diseases

### 3. Biological Control
- Use natural enemies of pests:
  - Parasitic wasps for cassava mealybug (released by IITA across Nigeria)
  - Predatory beetles for aphids
  - Trichoderma fungus against soil-borne diseases
- Encourage natural pest enemies by reducing unnecessary pesticide use
- Neem-based products (from neem tree leaves and seeds) repel many insects

### 4. Chemical Control (Last Resort)
- Use pesticides only when other methods are not sufficient
- Use recommended products at recommended doses
- Apply at the right time (early in infestation)
- Follow safety precautions
- Rotate pesticides with different active ingredients to prevent resistance

## Common Pests in Nigeria

### Fall Armyworm
- **Crops affected:** Maize (primary), rice, sorghum, millet
- **Identification:** Green-brown caterpillars with an inverted Y on the head; holes and frass in the leaf whorl
- **Control:** Early detection is critical. Apply recommended insecticides (Ampligo, Belt, Emamectin benzoate) into the leaf whorl. Remove and destroy heavily infested plants. Report to your extension worker.
- **Prevention:** Early planting, intercropping with beans

### Stem Borers
- **Crops affected:** Maize, rice, sorghum, millet, sugarcane
- **Identification:** Dead hearts (young plants), white heads (mature plants), bore holes in stems
- **Control:** Apply granular insecticides (Furadan) into the leaf whorl. Remove and destroy crop stubble after harvest.
- **Prevention:** Early planting, destroy crop residues, use resistant varieties

### Tuta Absoluta (Tomato Leaf Miner)
- **Crops affected:** Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant
- **Identification:** Small moth; larvae mine inside leaves and fruits, creating tunnels
- **Control:** Pheromone traps to monitor and catch moths. Apply recommended insecticides (Chlorantraniliprole, Spinosad). Remove and destroy affected plants.
- **Prevention:** Use insect-proof nets, crop rotation, remove wild solanaceous weeds

### Aphids
- **Crops affected:** Vegetables, beans, cowpea, cotton
- **Identification:** Small soft-bodied insects clustered on leaves and stems; sticky honeydew on leaves
- **Control:** Spray with neem-based products or insecticides (Imidacloprid, Lambda-cyhalothrin). Encourage ladybird beetles (natural predators).

### Whiteflies
- **Crops affected:** Tomatoes, cassava, beans, cotton
- **Identification:** Tiny white flying insects under leaves; transmit plant viruses
- **Control:** Yellow sticky traps, insecticides (Imidacloprid), neem spray
- **Prevention:** Use resistant varieties, remove weeds that harbour whiteflies

### Grasshoppers and Locusts
- **Crops affected:** All crops
- **Control:** Spray with recommended insecticides during outbreaks. Report locust swarms to your state Ministry of Agriculture immediately.

### Rodents (Rats and Mice)
- **Crops affected:** All stored grains, cassava, yam
- **Control:** Clear vegetation around farms and storage areas. Use traps. Use rodenticides carefully (keep away from food and children). Proper storage in sealed containers.

## Common Diseases

### Cassava Mosaic Disease
- Curled, distorted leaves with yellow and green mosaic pattern
- Spread by whiteflies
- Use resistant varieties (TME 419, TMS 98/0505). Remove and destroy infected plants. Control whiteflies.

### Rice Blast
- Brown spots on leaves, can destroy entire fields
- Apply fungicides (Tricyclazole, Carbendazim). Use resistant varieties.

### Maize Streak Virus
- Yellow streaks on maize leaves, stunting
- Transmitted by leafhoppers. Use resistant varieties. Control leafhoppers.

### Late Blight (Tomatoes and Potatoes)
- Water-soaked spots on leaves, white mould underneath, rapid wilting
- Apply fungicides (Ridomil Gold, Metalaxyl) preventively in wet weather. Use resistant varieties.

### Bacterial Wilt
- Sudden wilting and death of entire plant
- No chemical cure. Remove and destroy affected plants. Practice crop rotation (3-4 years). Use clean planting material.

## Pesticide Safety

### Before Application
- Read the label carefully — it tells you what to use, how much, when, and safety precautions
- Wear protective clothing: long sleeves, trousers, boots, gloves, face mask or nose cover
- Mix pesticides outdoors, away from food, water sources, and children
- Use the correct dose — more is not better and wastes money

### During Application
- Apply in the early morning or late evening (not midday) — avoids heat and wind
- Spray downwind — do not spray into the wind
- Do not eat, drink, or smoke while spraying
- Keep children and animals away from the sprayed area
- Do not spray when it is about to rain — rain washes off the pesticide

### After Application
- Wash hands, face, and body thoroughly with soap and water
- Wash all clothing worn during spraying
- Clean and store equipment properly
- Do not reuse pesticide containers for food or water — destroy them
- Observe the waiting period before harvest (printed on the label)
- Store pesticides in locked storage, away from food and children

### Poisoning Signs
If you or someone shows these signs after pesticide contact: dizziness, headache, nausea, vomiting, difficulty breathing, blurred vision, excessive sweating, muscle twitching — go to the hospital immediately.

## Neem-Based Pest Control (Organic Option)

Neem tree (Dongoyaro) products are effective against many pests and are safer than synthetic pesticides.

### How to Prepare Neem Solution
1. Collect 1 kg of mature neem leaves or 500g of neem seeds
2. Crush or grind them
3. Soak in 5 litres of water overnight
4. Strain through a cloth
5. Add a small amount of liquid soap (1 teaspoon) to help it stick to leaves
6. Spray on crops — effective against aphids, caterpillars, whiteflies, and many other insects
7. Reapply after rain

## Tips

- Scout your field regularly — walk through and check for pest damage at least weekly
- Early detection saves money — a small infestation is much cheaper to control than a large one
- Do not use banned pesticides (DDT, Endosulfan, Lindane) — they are harmful to health and environment
- Crop rotation is one of the most effective and cheapest pest control methods
- Keep records of pest problems and treatments — patterns help predict future outbreaks
- Report unusual pest outbreaks to your local extension worker or state ADP
