# Livestock Farming Basics in Nigeria

## Cattle (Cow) Rearing

### Types
- **Dairy cattle:** Holstein-Friesian, Jersey (mostly in commercial farms)
- **Beef cattle:** White Fulani (Bunaji), Red Bororo, Sokoto Gudali, N'Dama (trypanotolerant)
- **Dual purpose:** White Fulani is the most common breed in Nigeria

### Feeding
- Graze on natural pasture (most common system in Nigeria)
- Supplement with crop residues (maize stalks, groundnut haulms, cowpea hay)
- Provide salt licks and mineral supplements
- During the dry season, supplement with concentrates (wheat bran, cotton seed cake, groundnut cake)
- Always provide clean water

### Health
- Vaccinate against: Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR), Anthrax, Black Quarter
- Deworm every 3-4 months
- Dip or spray against ticks regularly
- Watch for trypanosomosis (spread by tsetse fly) — use preventive drugs or trypanotolerant breeds in tsetse areas
- Contact a veterinarian for any illness

### Tips
- Register with your local veterinary office
- Cattle rustling is a security concern — report to police and community vigilantes
- Consider improved breeds through artificial insemination (AI) — contact your state ADP
- Keep records of births, deaths, vaccinations, and sales

## Goat Farming

### Types
- **West African Dwarf (WAD):** Small, hardy, trypanotolerant, common in southern Nigeria
- **Red Sokoto (Maradi):** Medium-sized, good skin quality, common in the north
- **Sahel goat:** Large, common in the far north
- **Boer goat:** Imported meat breed, fast-growing, good for crossbreeding

### Feeding
- Browse on shrubs, trees, and weeds (goats prefer leaves to grass)
- Supplement with kitchen scraps, crop residues, concentrates
- Provide salt lick and clean water

### Housing
- Simple pen with roof, raised floor (to keep goats dry and prevent disease)
- Good ventilation
- Separate bucks (males) from does (females) to control breeding

### Health
- Vaccinate against PPR (Peste des Petits Ruminants) — the most deadly goat disease
- Deworm every 3 months
- Watch for foot rot (common in wet conditions) — keep the pen dry
- Treat for external parasites (ticks, lice, mange)

### Economics
- Very low startup cost — start with 5-10 does and 1 buck
- Goats breed prolifically — twins are common
- High demand during festive periods (Sallah, Christmas, Easter)
- Good investment for rural households and women

## Sheep Farming

### Types
- **West African Dwarf sheep:** Small, hardy, common in the south
- **Yankasa:** Medium, white with black markings, popular in the north
- **Uda:** Large, bicoloured, common in the northeast
- **Balami:** Largest Nigerian breed, white, common in the northeast

### Feeding
- Graze on grass and pasture
- Supplement with hay, crop residues, concentrates in dry season
- Salt lick and clean water

### Health
- Vaccinate against PPR
- Deworm regularly
- Sheep are susceptible to respiratory infections — provide shelter from rain and cold winds

### Tips
- Very high demand during Eid al-Adha (Sallah) — time your breeding to have market-sized rams available
- Rams can sell for ₦50,000-₦500,000+ during Sallah depending on breed and size

## Pig Farming

### Breeds
- **Large White:** Most popular in Nigeria, fast-growing
- **Landrace:** Good mothering ability
- **Duroc:** Hardy, good feed conversion
- **Local breeds:** Hardy but slow-growing

### Feeding
- Pigs eat almost everything: kitchen waste, grains, crop residues, commercial feed
- For best growth, use balanced commercial pig feed or well-formulated ration
- Pregnant and lactating sows need higher protein
- Always provide plenty of clean water

### Housing
- Concrete floor with good drainage and slope for easy cleaning
- Separate pens for: boars, sows, weaners, growers/finishers
- Provide shade and wallowing pit (mud) for cooling — pigs cannot sweat
- Good ventilation

### Health
- Vaccinate against African Swine Fever (ASF) — the most devastating pig disease in Nigeria (no effective vaccine yet — biosecurity is the only protection)
- Deworm regularly
- Practice strict biosecurity: do not allow visitors, vehicles, or new pigs without quarantine
- ASF has no cure — if it enters your farm, it can kill all your pigs

### Tips
- Pig farming is very profitable but requires good management
- Sows can produce 8-12 piglets per litter, 2 litters per year
- Market in some areas may be limited due to religious/cultural factors — know your market
- Processing (smoked pork, sausages) adds significant value

## General Livestock Tips

### Record Keeping
Keep records of:
- Number of animals (births, deaths, sales, purchases)
- Feeding (type, amount, cost)
- Health (vaccinations, treatments, diseases)
- Breeding (mating dates, births)
- Income and expenses

### Veterinary Services
- Register with your state veterinary services
- Find a nearby veterinarian or animal health worker
- Vaccinate on schedule — prevention is much cheaper than treatment
- Report unusual animal deaths to the veterinary authorities — it could be a disease outbreak

### Biosecurity
- Quarantine new animals for 2-4 weeks before introducing to the herd
- Keep your farm clean
- Control visitors and vehicles entering your farm
- Dispose of dead animals properly (bury deep or burn)
- Wash hands and change clothes/shoes when moving between animal groups

## Where to Get Help

- State Veterinary Services
- National Veterinary Research Institute (NVRI), Vom, Plateau State
- State Agricultural Development Programme (ADP)
- Federal Ministry of Agriculture
- Local animal health workers and veterinarians
