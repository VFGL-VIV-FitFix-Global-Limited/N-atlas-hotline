# Tomato Farming in Nigeria

## Overview

Tomatoes are one of the most important vegetable crops in Nigeria, used in virtually every Nigerian dish. Nigeria is the largest tomato producer in Africa, but post-harvest losses are extremely high (up to 50%), creating both a challenge and an opportunity for farmers.

## Varieties

### Open-Pollinated Varieties
- **UC 82B:** High yielding, compact plant, good for processing
- **Roma VF:** Good for paste production, resistant to some diseases
- **Tropimech:** Adapted to tropical conditions

### Hybrid Varieties (Higher yield but seeds cannot be saved)
- **Technisem varieties** (Cobra, Mongal, Padma)
- **East-West Seed varieties**
- **Syngenta varieties**

Hybrid seeds are more expensive but can yield 20-40 tonnes per hectare vs 10-15 tonnes for local varieties.

## Planting

### When to Plant
- Dry season (irrigated): October to February — best season for tomatoes in most of Nigeria
- Wet season: May to July — more disease pressure, requires good management
- Year-round production is possible with irrigation

### Nursery
1. Prepare a raised nursery bed (1m wide, any length)
2. Mix topsoil with well-decomposed manure
3. Sow seeds in shallow drills (1 cm deep), 10 cm between rows
4. Cover lightly with soil and mulch with dry grass
5. Water gently morning and evening
6. Provide partial shade for the first week
7. Seedlings are ready for transplanting at 3-4 weeks (when 10-15 cm tall with 4-6 leaves)
8. Harden seedlings by reducing watering 3-4 days before transplanting

### Transplanting
- Transplant in the evening or on cloudy days to reduce transplant shock
- Spacing: 60 cm between rows, 40-50 cm between plants
- Water immediately after transplanting
- Apply mulch around plants

## Crop Management

### Watering
- Tomatoes need regular, consistent watering
- Drip irrigation is most efficient and reduces disease
- Water at the base of the plant, not on the leaves
- Inconsistent watering causes blossom end rot and fruit cracking

### Fertiliser
- **Before transplanting:** Work well-decomposed manure or compost into the soil
- **At transplanting:** NPK 15:15:15 at 300 kg per hectare
- **3 weeks after transplanting:** Urea or CAN at 150 kg per hectare
- **At flowering:** NPK 15:15:15 or potassium-rich fertiliser (to improve fruit quality)
- Tomatoes are heavy feeders — fertilise regularly for good yields

### Staking and Pruning
- Stake plants to keep fruits off the ground (reduces rot and disease)
- Use wooden stakes or string trellis
- Remove suckers (side shoots from leaf axils) for determinate varieties
- Prune lower leaves that touch the ground

### Weed Control
- Keep the field weed-free, especially in the first 6 weeks
- Mulching reduces weeds and conserves moisture
- Hand weeding or herbicides (Sencor/metribuzin for pre-emergence)

## Pests and Diseases

### Major Pests
- **Fruit worm (Helicoverpa):** Bores into fruits. Spray with recommended insecticides (Lambda-cyhalothrin, Emamectin benzoate)
- **Whiteflies:** Transmit viruses, suck sap. Use yellow sticky traps and insecticides (Imidacloprid)
- **Leaf miners:** Create tunnels in leaves. Spray with Abamectin
- **Tuta absoluta (tomato leaf miner):** Devastating pest. Use pheromone traps, remove affected plants, apply recommended insecticides

### Major Diseases
- **Early blight:** Brown spots with concentric rings on leaves. Apply fungicides (Mancozeb, Ridomil Gold)
- **Late blight:** Water-soaked spots, white mould under leaves. Apply Ridomil Gold or Metalaxyl
- **Bacterial wilt:** Sudden wilting and death. No chemical cure — remove affected plants, rotate crops, use resistant varieties
- **Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV):** Leaf curling and yellowing. Control whiteflies (the vector). Use resistant varieties.

### Prevention
- Rotate tomatoes with non-solanaceous crops (not peppers, eggplant, or potatoes)
- Do not grow tomatoes in the same field more than 2 seasons in a row
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Use drip irrigation instead of overhead watering
- Apply fungicides preventively during wet weather

## Harvesting

- Tomatoes are ready for harvest 60-90 days after transplanting (depending on variety)
- Harvest when fruits are mature green (for long-distance transport) or red/ripe (for local sale)
- Harvest early in the morning when fruits are cool
- Handle gently to avoid bruising — damaged fruits rot quickly
- Harvest every 2-3 days during peak production

## Post-Harvest Handling

Post-harvest losses in Nigeria are enormous — up to 50% of tomatoes are lost between farm and consumer.

### Reducing Losses
- Harvest carefully — do not throw or pile fruits
- Use plastic crates instead of baskets (baskets crush bottom fruits)
- Transport in the evening or early morning (not in midday heat)
- Sell quickly — fresh tomatoes last only 5-7 days
- Consider processing: tomato paste, dried tomatoes, tomato powder

### Processing
- **Sun-dried tomatoes:** Slice and dry in the sun — stores for months and sells at premium
- **Tomato paste:** Cook, sieve, and concentrate — requires some equipment but very profitable
- **Frozen tomatoes:** Blend and freeze — requires cold chain but growing market

## Economics

- **Cost of production:** ₦300,000-₦600,000 per hectare
- **Expected yield:** 15-40 tonnes per hectare (depending on variety and management)
- **Selling price:** ₦50,000-₦200,000 per tonne (highly variable — prices can swing 5x between glut and scarcity)
- **Best prices:** September to November (off-season, scarce supply)
- **Lowest prices:** January to March (peak harvest, oversupply)

## Tips

- Timing is everything — grow in the dry season with irrigation for best results and best prices
- Invest in drip irrigation — it saves water, reduces disease, and increases yield
- Tuta absoluta is the biggest threat — monitor constantly and act early
- Do not overuse pesticides — it kills beneficial insects and creates resistance
- Process your tomatoes during glut season to avoid losses and capture value
- Join a tomato farmers' group for bulk purchasing of inputs and collective marketing
- Consider a tomato processing business (paste, dried tomato) — the market is huge
