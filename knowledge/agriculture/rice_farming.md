# Rice Farming in Nigeria

## Overview

Rice is one of the most important food crops in Nigeria. Nigeria is the largest rice consumer in Africa, and the government actively promotes local rice production to reduce imports. Rice farming can be very profitable with the right variety, inputs, and management.

## Rice Ecosystems in Nigeria

### Rainfed Upland Rice
- Grown on dry land (not flooded), depends on rainfall
- Suitable for most of southern and middle-belt Nigeria
- Lower yields but easier to manage
- Popular areas: Ogun, Ekiti, Ondo, Benue, Nasarawa

### Rainfed Lowland Rice
- Grown in low-lying areas that collect rainwater
- Natural flooding provides water
- Higher yields than upland
- Popular areas: Niger, Kaduna, Kebbi, Benue, Ebonyi

### Irrigated Rice
- Grown with controlled water supply from dams, canals, or pumps
- Highest yields — can be grown year-round (dry and wet season)
- Popular areas: Kebbi, Sokoto, Jigawa, Kano, Niger, Anambra

## Recommended Varieties

- **FARO 44 (SIPI):** Popular in the north, good yield, long grain
- **FARO 52 (WITA 4):** High yielding, lowland, medium grain
- **FARO 66:** High yielding, suitable for irrigated and lowland
- **NERICA 1 and 2:** For upland, early maturing (90-100 days), drought-tolerant
- **Ofada rice:** Popular in the southwest, aromatic, premium price
- Check with your state ADP or National Cereals Research Institute (NCRI) for varieties suited to your location

## Planting

### When to Plant
- Wet season (main crop): June to August
- Dry season (irrigated only): November to February

### Land Preparation
1. Clear the field
2. Plough and harrow (or use manual labour)
3. Level the field — this is very important for even water distribution
4. For lowland/irrigated: construct bunds (small embankments) around the field to hold water
5. Puddling (for transplanted rice): flood the field and plough wet to create a smooth muddy surface

### Two Planting Methods

**Direct Seeding (Broadcasting or Drilling)**
- Sow seeds directly into the prepared field
- Seed rate: 60-80 kg per hectare (broadcasting) or 40-50 kg (drilling in rows)
- Easier but more difficult to weed

**Transplanting (Recommended for higher yields)**
1. Prepare a nursery: sow seeds in a small well-prepared bed
2. Raise seedlings for 21-30 days
3. Transplant seedlings to the main field at 2-3 seedlings per hill
4. Spacing: 20cm x 20cm or 25cm x 25cm
5. Transplant in lines for easier weeding

## Crop Management

### Water Management
- Upland rice: depends on rainfall — cannot survive prolonged drought or flooding
- Lowland rice: maintain 5-10cm of water during most of the growing period
- Irrigated rice: controlled flooding and draining at different growth stages
- Drain the field 2-3 weeks before harvest

### Fertiliser Application
- **Basal application (at planting):** NPK 15:15:15 at 200 kg per hectare
- **First top-dressing (3-4 weeks after planting/transplanting):** Urea at 50 kg per hectare
- **Second top-dressing (at panicle initiation, about 8 weeks):** Urea at 50 kg per hectare
- Apply fertiliser when the soil is moist
- Do not apply fertiliser when the field is dry or flooded too deeply

### Weed Control
- Weeds are the biggest yield reducer in rice farming
- First weeding: 2-3 weeks after planting
- Second weeding: 5-6 weeks after planting
- Herbicides: Pre-emergence (Butachlor, Pendimethalin) applied just after planting; Post-emergence (2,4-D) for broadleaf weeds
- Hand weeding is effective but labour-intensive

## Pests and Diseases

### Major Pests
- **Stem borers:** Bore into rice stems, causing "dead hearts" or "white heads." Remove and destroy affected plants; apply recommended insecticides
- **Rice weevils:** Attack stored grain. Dry rice properly before storage; use airtight containers
- **Birds (quelea):** Damage ripening grain. Use bird-scaring methods; harvest promptly when mature
- **Rodents:** Damage crops in the field and in storage. Clear vegetation around the field; use traps

### Major Diseases
- **Rice blast:** Brown spots on leaves; use resistant varieties and recommended fungicides
- **Bacterial leaf blight:** Yellowing and wilting of leaves; use resistant varieties
- **Rice yellow mottle virus (RYMV):** Yellowing and stunting; use resistant varieties and control insect vectors

## Harvesting

- Rice is ready for harvest when 80-90% of the grains on the panicle are golden/straw-coloured
- Upland rice: typically 90-120 days after planting
- Lowland/irrigated rice: typically 110-140 days
- Harvest by cutting the panicles or the whole plant
- Thresh immediately or within 24 hours to reduce grain loss
- Dry to 12-14% moisture content before storage

## Post-Harvest Processing

1. **Threshing:** Separate grains from the panicle (manual or machine threshing)
2. **Winnowing:** Remove chaff and debris
3. **Drying:** Dry to 12-14% moisture (sun drying on mats or tarpaulin, or use a dryer)
4. **Milling:** Remove husk and bran to produce white rice (use a rice mill)
5. **Grading and packaging:** Sort by grain quality and package for sale

Proper drying and milling are critical for grain quality and market price.

## Economics

- **Cost of production:** ₦200,000-₦500,000 per hectare (depending on method and inputs)
- **Expected yield:** 3-6 tonnes per hectare (paddy)
- **Selling price:** ₦300,000-₦500,000 per tonne (milled rice, varies by location and quality)
- **Ofada rice:** Premium price, up to ₦600,000+ per tonne

## Support and Programmes

- **Anchor Borrowers' Programme (ABP):** CBN-backed programme providing loans and inputs to rice farmers
- **State ADPs:** Provide extension services, training, and sometimes subsidised inputs
- **National Cereals Research Institute (NCRI):** Badeggi, Niger State — provides improved varieties and advice
- **Rice Farmers Association of Nigeria (RIFAN):** Advocacy and support for rice farmers

## Tips

- Use certified improved seeds — the yield difference is 2-3 times higher
- Level your field properly — uneven fields waste water and reduce yield
- Do not skip fertiliser — rice is a heavy feeder
- Weed early and on time — late weeding loses yield
- Dry and store properly — poor post-harvest handling wastes your effort
- Join a rice farmers' cooperative for better access to inputs, processing, and markets
- Consider dry-season irrigated rice — it is often more profitable due to less competition
