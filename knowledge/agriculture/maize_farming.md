# Maize (Corn) Farming in Nigeria

## Overview

Maize is one of the most widely grown cereal crops in Nigeria, used for food, animal feed, and industrial purposes. It grows across all agroecological zones of Nigeria and can be grown twice a year in areas with two rainy seasons.

## Varieties

### Improved Varieties
- **SAMMAZ varieties** (e.g., SAMMAZ 14, SAMMAZ 15, SAMMAZ 27) — developed by the Institute for Agricultural Research (IAR), Zaria
- **OBA SUPER** varieties — popular in southwestern Nigeria
- **Drought-tolerant varieties** (DTMA) — developed by IITA, suitable for drier areas
- **Striga-resistant varieties** — for areas affected by striga weed (common in the north)

Improved varieties yield 3-6 tonnes per hectare vs 1-2 tonnes for local varieties.

## Planting

### When to Plant
- Early season: March to April (southern Nigeria), May to June (northern Nigeria)
- Late season: August to September (southern Nigeria, where applicable)

### Land Preparation
1. Clear the land
2. Plough and harrow, or prepare ridges
3. Ridges help with drainage and root development

### Seed Rate and Spacing
- Seed rate: 20-25 kg per hectare
- Spacing: 75cm between rows, 25cm between plants (for 2 seeds per hole)
- Plant 2 seeds per hole, thin to 1 plant per stand after 2 weeks
- Plant depth: 3-5 cm

### Planting Steps
1. Make ridges or flat seedbed
2. Mark planting positions at correct spacing
3. Drop 2 seeds per hole
4. Cover lightly with soil
5. Thin to 1 plant per stand after 2 weeks

## Crop Management

### Fertiliser Application
- **At planting:** NPK 15:15:15 at 100 kg per hectare (or 2-3 bottle caps per stand, placed 5-8 cm away from the plant)
- **4-6 weeks after planting:** Urea at 100 kg per hectare as top dressing (or 1-2 bottle caps per stand)
- Apply when soil is moist
- Do not place fertiliser directly on the plant — it will burn

### Weeding
- First weeding: 2-3 weeks after planting
- Second weeding: 5-6 weeks after planting
- Herbicides: Atrazine or Primextra (pre-emergence), Gramozone (post-emergence for inter-row weeds)
- Keep the field weed-free for the first 6 weeks — this is the critical period

### Water
- Maize needs adequate rainfall or irrigation, especially during tasselling (flowering) and grain filling
- Water stress during flowering can reduce yield by 50% or more
- In irrigated areas, water regularly during dry spells

## Pests and Diseases

### Major Pests
- **Fall Armyworm:** The most destructive pest of maize in Nigeria. Look for holes and frass (droppings) in the leaf whorl. Apply recommended insecticides (e.g., Ampligo, Belt) early. Report to your extension worker.
- **Stem borers:** Bore into stems, causing "dead hearts." Apply granular insecticides into the leaf whorl.
- **Weevils (storage):** Damage stored grain. Dry grain properly and use hermetic (airtight) storage bags like PICS bags.

### Major Diseases
- **Maize streak virus:** Yellow streaks on leaves, stunted growth. Use resistant varieties. Control leafhopper vectors.
- **Downy mildew:** White fuzzy growth on leaves. Use resistant varieties and remove infected plants.
- **Striga (parasitic weed):** Attaches to maize roots and steals nutrients. Use Striga-resistant varieties. Apply herbicide-coated seeds (Imazapyr-resistant maize — "StrigAway").

## Harvesting

- Maize is ready for harvest 90-120 days after planting (depending on variety)
- Harvest when husks are dry and brown, and kernels are hard
- For fresh maize (green maize): harvest earlier when kernels are soft and milky (70-80 days)
- Remove cobs from the plant by hand
- Dry cobs in the sun before shelling

## Post-Harvest Handling

1. **Drying:** Dry cobs or shelled grain to 12-13% moisture content
2. **Shelling:** Remove kernels from cobs (manual or machine)
3. **Storage:** Store in clean, dry bags or hermetic (airtight) bags (PICS bags) to prevent weevil damage
4. **Do not store wet grain** — it will develop aflatoxin (a dangerous mould toxin)

### Aflatoxin Prevention
- Dry maize properly before storage
- Do not store damaged or mouldy cobs
- Use proper storage (PICS bags, clean dry rooms)
- Sort and remove discoloured or damaged grains
- Aflatoxin is invisible and dangerous — it causes liver cancer

## Economics

- **Cost of production:** ₦120,000-₦250,000 per hectare
- **Expected yield:** 3-6 tonnes per hectare with improved varieties
- **Selling price:** ₦150,000-₦300,000 per tonne (dry grain)
- **Green maize:** Selling fresh maize is highly profitable near urban markets

## Tips

- Plant improved varieties — the yield difference is dramatic
- Do not skip fertiliser — maize is a heavy feeder
- Watch for fall armyworm — early detection and treatment are critical
- Dry your grain properly before storage — aflatoxin contamination is a serious health and economic risk
- PICS bags are the cheapest and most effective storage solution for smallholder farmers
- Rotate maize with legumes (beans, soybeans, groundnuts) to improve soil fertility and reduce pests
